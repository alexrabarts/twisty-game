# Rendering

All rendering goes through `Graphics` in `js/graphics.js`. `main.js` never calls `renderer.render` for the game scene.

## Colour

- `THREE.ColorManagement.enabled` is set at the top of `graphics.js`, before any `Color` exists. Hex and CSS colours are sRGB. Lighting runs in linear space.
- `Graphics.fixColorSpaces()` tags `map` and `emissiveMap` textures as sRGB and decodes float vertex colours once. It runs when a leg starts and again every 1.5 s, because particles and cones are created during a leg.
- Author float vertex colours in sRGB. Do not decode them yourself.
- `Color.setRGB` and `Color.setHSL` take linear values unless you pass `THREE.SRGBColorSpace`.

## Frame

1. The scene renders into a 4x MSAA half-float target. The renderer does no tone mapping.
2. Bloom: threshold, five-level blur chain, additive upsample. The threshold is 1.0, or the preset's `threshold` (night: 1.8, with bloom 0.6), so at night only lamp heads, the moon and the middle of a lit window of the modelled village bloom (the window by little: its glass is 1.9 to 2.3); headlit paint does not.
3. Composite: radial speed blur, height fog, light shafts, bloom, ACES tone map, saturation, contrast, tint, vignette, sRGB encode, dither.

`renderer.toneMappingExposure` is still the exposure control. `Game.writeTime` sets it.

If the browser has no WebGL2 float render targets, `postEnabled` is false. The renderer then tone-maps to the canvas, and the CSS speed overlay in `main.js` is used.

## Depth atmosphere

`Graphics.setAtmosphere(atmosphere, render, baseY)` takes `{ heightFog, shafts }`, `{ ring }` and `baseY`, the lowest road height of the part. Pass `null` to reset. Only `Game.writeAtmosphere` calls it, from a look sampled off the atmosphere track (see "Atmosphere along the road"). It runs before the sky is written: `writeSky` reads `ringStrength`.

- **Depth texture.** `sceneTarget` has a `DepthTexture` (`DepthFormat`, `UnsignedIntType`) set in the constructor options. Rules: set it before the first render into the target and never attach it later; never use `DepthStencilFormat`; sample depth only in a pass that draws to another target (the composite pass draws to the canvas); `setSize` resizes it with the target.
- **Guard.** `atmosphereSupported` is false when `postEnabled` is false or the renderer has `WEBGL_multisampled_render_to_texture` (three r150 takes a different branch there). Then there is no depth texture, no `shaftTarget`, and height fog and shafts stay off. Material `FogExp2` still applies, so the game looks as before.
- **Height fog.** It lives in the composite pass: no new full-resolution pass or target. The shader rebuilds the world position from `tDepth`, `uProjInv` and `uCamWorld`, integrates a density that falls off exponentially with height along the view ray, and mixes toward `uFogColor` (`scene.fog.color`). It runs after the speed blur and before bloom and the tone map. Sky pixels (depth 1) reach `uFogReach`, which makes the mist band at the horizon. `heightFog` is `{ density, falloff }`: density per metre at `baseY`, falloff per metre of climb.
- **Light shafts.** `shaftTarget` is a half-float target at a quarter of the scene target's size. One pass marches 24 steps toward the sun's screen position and gathers bright sky pixels. The composite adds the result. `shaftsActive` is false when `atmosphere.shafts` is 0, the sun is behind the camera or the time of day uses the moon. Shafts are on for legs 1, 2, 4 and 5.
- **Far ring.** `Graphics.ringStrength` (default 1, `render.ring`) multiplies `uMountains` in `applySky` and in the thick-fog branch of `update`. The environment bake keeps it at 0. Only coastal-descent differs (0.35). A look with `ring: [from, to]` (mountain-dawn, from `render.ringRise`: 0.15 to 1) ramps the ring over its part; each frame `Game.applyAtmosphere` calls `Graphics.setRingStrength()` with the value for the bike's progress, and the ring reaches `to` at the end of the part.
- **Cull distance and shadows.** `main.js` clamps the cull distance to `render.cullMax` (600, or 800 on mountain-dawn, coastal-descent and winter-pass). `applyShadowBox` sets the sun's shadow box to `render.shadowSpan` wide (120, or 200 on foggy-gorge, with the shadow far plane at least 300 and twice the span). The shadow map stays 2048.
- **Weather.** `WeatherSystem.baseY` is the same `baseY`; `updateFogDensity` thins the fog above it. `WeatherSystem.writeFogDensity` is the only writer of `scene.fog.density` on a ride: the fog of the part, replaced by the weather's fog by its share.

## Atmosphere along the road

`js/atmosphere.js` holds `AtmosphereTrack`: plain data, no scene access. A leg is a list of parts, and each part has one look: `{ timeOfDay, weather, weatherIntensity, fogDensity, heightFog, shafts, ring, baseY, shadowSpan }`. A leg with `config.parts` is a track of those parts. Every other leg is a track of one part, made by `AtmosphereTrack.forLeg`, so all legs use one code path.

- **Blend zone.** `sample(segment)` gives `{ a, b, t, u }`. The zone is 15 segments (300 m) wide, centred on each part boundary, with a smoothstep weight. Outside it `t` is exactly 0 and `a === b`. Two zones may not touch.
- **Resolve and write.** `Graphics.resolveSky` and `Game.resolveTime` return plain values, the same object for the same arguments. `blendSky` and `blendTime` mix two of them. `writeSky` and `writeTime` copy them into existing objects, so a frame allocates no `THREE.Color`. What has no middle (the moon, the names) switches at `t = 0.5`.
- **Two entry points.** `Game.snapAtmosphere(track, segment, { environment, weatherSystem })` writes everything at once, in this order: ring and height fog, light, sky (it marks the environment for a bake), then the road surface and the tint of the ground (`environment`), then the particles, base height and fog density (`weatherSystem`). A leg start, a checkpoint restart, the menu preview and the rider showcase use it. With `weatherSystem: null` it writes no particles and no fog density. `Game.applyAtmosphere` runs once per rendered frame: on a part it writes only the far ring; in a zone it blends light, sky, road surface and weather; the first frame after a zone writes the part's own values.
- **Fixed leg-start order.** `applyLandscapeConfig` (it writes the part's fog density), `applyShadowBox`, build the track, `new WeatherSystem`, `prepare`, `snapAtmosphere`, then cones, rockfalls and traffic. The light moved after the environment build: no builder reads the time of day, the fog colour or `Graphics` state while it builds. If one does, the `atmostate` ride arm fails.
- **The preview and the showcase.** The menu preview snaps to the first settled segment of the part that holds the orbit pivot, with no weather system: its fog density is the one `applyLandscapeConfig` wrote. The rider showcase snaps a one-look track (golden, clear, no height fog, ring 1), then sets its own fog colour and density `0.0018`.
- **`Game.setTimeOfDay(key)`** changes the light and sky time only, in clear air, and sets `atmosphereHold`. While the hold is set `applyAtmosphere` writes the ring only. The next snap clears it. Only `tools/village-shots.mjs` calls it.
- **Rebake.** `Graphics.requestRebake` asks for a bake of the environment map only if 30 frames passed since the last one and the blend moved 0.125 since it. Outside a zone nothing bakes. `graphics.stats = { envBakes, lastBakeFrame }` counts them.
- **Weather.** `WeatherSystem.prepare(types)` makes the particle systems once per leg. `setMix({ rain, snow, fog, intensity, fogDensity, baseY })` changes only the draw range, the fog colour and density, and the grip. It allocates nothing. `weatherType` is the weather with the largest share. Grip is blended by share; a share of 1 gives today's multipliers exactly. `Game.initializeGameWithLeg` disposes the old weather system before it makes a new one.
- **Random source.** `WeatherSystem` takes every random number from `this.random`. A one-part track passes `Math.random`, so cones, rockfalls and traffic read the same stream as before. A track of several parts passes `AtmosphereTrack.seededRandom(boardId)`.
- **Fog thickness.** `Graphics.update` blends the thick-fog sky by `fogShare` (the share of fog in the sky), so a fog-to-clear boundary does not jump. `Game.updateCullDistance` reads the fog density again in every zone frame.
- **Looks left out.** A part that leaves out a field takes it from the part before it. The weather defaults to clear, and an intensity left out is 1.0. A tunnel or gallery part can therefore name only its fog.
- **A world's looks.** `js/world/looks.js` (`WorldLooks`) holds the parts of each stage as shares of its length; `AtmosphereTrack.forWorld` lays them along the ride in 100 m units, so a blend is 1.5 km. Every stage of `world/manifest.json` changes its weather or its time of day at least once (`AtmosphereTrack.changesWithin`; `tests/unit/world-looks.test.js` reads the manifest, so a new stage fails until it has a look). The Tremola: clear from Airolo, a thin mist (fog 0.15) from 25% (s 3.3 km), rain 1.0 from 53% (s 6.99 km, 1,677 m), the first drops at s 6.24 km and full rain from s 7.74 km, before the cobbled hairpins stack (7.8 km). `?worldLook=dry` and `?worldLook=cloud` ride other looks.
- **Weather that ends.** A part of a stage must be longer than the blend (`AtmosphereTrack.build` throws on a shorter one), so a stage that goes from rain back to clear air gives each part more than 1.5 km on its shortest route. The Tremola's rain ends at 0.8 of the stage: the air is clear and the road dry for the last 1.9 km to the summit (`tests/unit/world-looks.test.js` holds the last kilometre dry).
- **A world's rain.** The weather system takes `WorldLooks.grip` (rain lateral 0.942, braking 0.8, acceleration 0.9 at intensity 1) and `WorldLooks.rain` (the legs' 3000 drops in a box 90 m across, not 200 m, wider and brighter streaks, no lightning); the legs keep `WeatherSystem.GRIP` and `WeatherSystem.RAIN`. The hairpin rule (P46): on a part with a hairpin, weather lateral grip x surface grip is 0.80 or more (`WorldLooks.hairpinGrip`, the lowside boundary at full lock). Wet cobbles give 0.942 x 0.85 = 0.8007; wet asphalt 0.942. Braking on wet cobbles is 0.8 x 0.85 = 0.68 of dry tarmac (dry cobbles 0.85). Under a world's rain cloud the sun and the fill light keep 0.1 (`Game.WORLD_OVERCAST_SUN`; at the legs' 0.25 the fill glinted on the wet road) and the headlight is on at 0.5 (`Game.WORLD_OVERCAST_HEADLIGHT`). `Game.prewarmWorldWeather` compiles the rain and the headlight's light count at the start. Traffic lamps do not change (`js/traffic.js`).

## Sky

- `SKY_PRESETS` has one look for each time of day. `SKY_WEATHER` overrides it for rain, snow and fog.
- The horizon colour is always `scene.fog.color`. Distant geometry fades into the sky because of this. Change the fog colour, not the sky, to change the horizon.
- Gradient and clouds are baked into a 1024 x 512 panorama every 8 frames. The dome samples it and adds the sun or moon disc, stars and two far mountain ranges.
- The same sky is baked with PMREM into `scene.environment` when a snap writes the sky, and, in a blend zone, at the pace of `requestRebake`.
- The sky mesh has `userData.isSky`. `setupPerformanceCulling` must skip it, and any mesh with `userData.skipCulling` (the instanced forest, the headlight beam).

## Rock face

On a leg with `rockFace` in `SceneAssets.LEGS` (foggy-gorge), the wall ribbon's material gets `onBeforeCompile` (`rockShader` in `js/sceneassets.js`): it replaces three's `map_fragment` and `normal_fragment_maps`. The vertex colours stay the look; a tiling detail map and normal map are read in shifting patches at a level distance and world height, so no part of a wall repeats and the beds stay level. `tools/blender/README.md` ("Rock face detail") has the rules and the cost.

## Lights

`timeConfigs` in `main.js` holds the light values. They are tuned for linear lighting: the sky light (hemisphere top) is the zenith colour, not the horizon colour.

`headlight` in a config switches on the one spot light and the visible beam cone. They are off in daylight because a spot light costs every lit fragment. The spot reaches 120 m with decay 2: a near-flat falloff lit a white house 60 m ahead as brightly as the road under the wheel, and the bloom turned it into a halo.

- **Headlight.** The bike's lamp is one spot light and nothing else: no cone, no second light (`tests/unit/headlight-dip.test.js` holds the count). It rides on the main beam with a clear road and dips while a car or a rider is in front, going either way (`Traffic.inBeam`: within `rangeM` ahead inside the beam's cone; `Traffic.stepDip`: down over `dipS`, held `holdS` after the road clears, up over `riseS`; `Traffic.BEAM` has the aim, reach and strength of each beam). `Game` writes the light's target, distance and intensity from the dip each frame.

## Cost

The game is fill-rate bound on high-DPI screens. `adaptResolution` lowers the pixel ratio below 52 fps. It ignores the first 4 s of a leg, when shaders compile.

## Bikes and rider

`Vehicle.createMesh` builds the procedural bike and rider, then calls `BikeModels.dress` (`js/bikemodels.js`). That swaps in the Blender-made model: a character's detailed bike from `assets/bikes/detail/` (`BIKE_MODELS`, built by `tools/blender/bikes/`), or a procedural kind's file from `assets/bikes/` (see `tools/blender/README.md`). `new Vehicle(scene, onScore, character, bikeId)` rides `bikeId` if the character owns it, else their first bike.

- **When.** Nothing loads with the menu. The rider select builds every rider's bike with `{ model: 'far' }` and dresses only the focused one (`Game.focusRiderShowcase`), so it fetches that bike and the rider. `BikeModels.preload(ids)` fetches the rider, the light rider and the bikes named with their far files (each character's first by default), for the probes. If the files are in when a vehicle is built, the swap is immediate.
- **Stand-in.** A bike that is shown but not looked at (`{ model: 'far' }`, `BikeModels.standIn`) is drawn from its own file's farthest level (`lod1`) with the light rider, when that file is in memory; it fetches nothing. So a bike is procedural (its `kind`) only the first time it is shown, before its file is in. `vehicle.far` is true on a stand-in, and `BikeModels.dress` replaces it with the full model. `{ model: false }` stays procedural always (the tests' bikes).
- **Levels.** Each part of a detailed bike is a `THREE.LOD`: `lod0` near, `lod1` beyond `BikeModels.FAR` (30 m), switched by the renderer. The brake lens keeps `lod0` only, because `updateMesh` reads its material. A third, lighter level is a file of its own, `assets/bikes/far/<id>.glb` (`BIKE_MODELS[id].far`): the bike as the crew ride it, at most 2,300 triangles (about 2,020 now) in five nodes (`body` with the forks, bar and headlight folded in, the two wheels with their discs, `brake_light`, `luggage`) and at most six materials a node. It is a separate file because the crew need five other bikes and none of their near levels: a far file is about 63 KB, a detail file about 800 KB. The ridden bike does not use it: it is never that far from its own camera. `tools/blender/far.py` makes it from the detail file's `lod1` by one rule for every bike.
- **Axles and pose.** A detailed bike sets `vehicle.axles` (rear and front `[y, z, radius]`) from its wheels; `updateMesh` spins each wheel by its own radius and the wheelie pivots on that rear axle. The rider's hip sits on the file's `seat` mark (or the entry's `seat`), in the entry's `pose` (lean, style), hands on `grip`, feet on `peg`; `riderSpec` and `riderBasePos` follow.
- **Handedness.** Three.js puts +x on the rider's left. The procedural bike is mirrored as it is built (`Vehicle.mirrorParts`: every part but the rider, pose unmirrored), and the files written the other way are mirrored as they load.
- **Colours.** A detailed bike keeps its file's colours; its cloned `paint` becomes `palette.paint`, `frame.material` and the colour `bikeColor` restores after a tint. A procedural kind's file still takes the palette. If they arrive later, the swap happens then, but only if `update()` has not run for that vehicle. A bike that is on the road keeps its procedural model until the next `reset()`. A file that fails to load, or a model that lacks a part, leaves the procedural bike.
- **Contract.** The swap sets the same fields on the vehicle (`frontWheel`, `rearWheel`, `frontDisc`, `rearDisc`, `leftFork`, `rightFork`, `handlebar`, `headlight`, `brakeLight`, `frame`, `fuelTank`, `rider`, `helmet`, `visor`, the limbs, and `luggage` where the file has it, shown if the entry says so). `frame.material` is the palette's paint material, so the crash and wheelie tints work. `brakeLight` has its own material. `vehicle.modelled` is true after a swap, and `vehicle.onModel` is called.
- **Rider.** The rider is a group of rigid segments, not a skinned mesh. `BikeModels` poses them once: the torso leans as `riderSpec.torsoLean` says, the hands go to the bike's `grip` mark and the feet to its `peg` mark. Elbows and knees are placed by the fixed lengths of the limbs. `updateMesh` moves and turns the rider group as before.
- **Materials.** Every modelled mesh has baked occlusion in its vertex colours (normalised bytes, which `fixColorSpaces` leaves alone) and its material has `vertexColors` on. Do not put a palette material on a mesh without a `color` attribute after a swap: it draws black.
- **Handlebar.** The front end and the hands are drawn at `Vehicle.shownBar`, not at the steering input: against the roll while the bike rolls (`Vehicle.BAR.counter` for each rad/s, so to roll left the bar goes right), a little into the turn at a held lean (the angle the front wheel needs for that radius, at most `intoMax`), and with the steering angle under `directSpeed`. It reads the lean only and the physics reads none of it (`tests/unit/bike-counter-steer.test.js`). A bike drawn any other way (a crew bike, a new kind) takes its bar from the same function.
- **Body lean.** A character's `bodyLean` (0 to 1) is how far the rider's body goes into a turn past the bike's own lean (`Vehicle.riderLean`, `Vehicle.BODY`): the rider's own roll and a move across the seat. 0 sits in line with the bike at any lean, 1 hangs off. `Vehicle.holdRider` then puts the hands back on the grips and the feet on the pegs, so a new rider or a new bike needs only the number. Drawn only (`tests/unit/rider-lean.test.js`). A rider of the crew leans by its character's number too (`AIMotorcycle.leanRider`, which calls the same `Vehicle.riderLean` and `Vehicle.holdRider`).
- **A fall as it happens.** `Vehicle.worldCrash(cause, fall)` takes how the crash is drawn: `'lowside'` and `'tip'` (a fall at walking pace) play out (`Vehicle.stepFall` with the physics' own time, `Vehicle.drawFall`, `Vehicle.FALL`): the bike goes from its lean to its side over `downS` (`tipS`), turns into the bend as it slides (`spin` for each metre), the rider comes off and drops behind on the line of the slide, and `Game` throws sparks on the carriageway or dust on the land. Any other crash lies on its side at once. The crash, the slide and the restart are the physics', unchanged (`tests/unit/crash-lowside.test.js`). A new kind of crash that should play out names its own `fall` and draws from the same state.
- **Crew.** The touring crew are the characters the player did not pick (`Traffic.crewOf`, by name, in the order of `CHARACTERS`), under their own names. Each rides its own bike (`BikeModels.crewBike`: the character's first) from that bike's far file, in the file's colours, and wears its first outfit, in its `build`, on the light rider (`rider_far.glb`), seated on the bike's own marks by the same `seat` and `pose` as the player's rider (`Vehicle.riderSpec` makes the spec for both). `AIMotorcycle` calls `BikeModels.crew`; its own procedural bike stands until the two files are in, and stays if one fails. Each rides at the pace of its own traits (`CHARACTERS[*].traits`, `Traffic.PACES`): see "The crew's pace" in [world.md](world.md). The files are asked for when the crew are made (the light rider and five far bikes, about 480 KB), not earlier: with them asked for at the start of a world, `world-budget` once stood 70,000 over with 659 trees in a window that holds about 310 (taken to be the tree models held back by six more requests; not proven). A rider already on the road when they arrive takes its bike when it is hidden or more than `AIMotorcycle.UNSEEN_M` (120 m) from the player (`modelWaiting`, `takeModel`); on a later ride of the same page the files are in memory and the crew start on their own bikes. A swap draws nothing from `Math.random`. Cost: a crew rider is about 3,100 triangles (bike 2,020, rider 1,010 to 1,220) in under 60 meshes, and five are about 15,500. Before, a rider on the one light sport bike was 6,152 once its files were in, and 2,576 as the procedural bike, which is what the window's budget probe counted (four of them, 10,300); `Scenery.TRIANGLE_CAP` went from 134,000 to 128,000 to pay for the five (5,200 over the four the probe counted). `tests/unit/crew.test.js` reads `CHARACTERS`, `BIKE_MODELS`, `OUTFITS` and the files, and holds all of this: a far file for each bike, a light twin for each cut, the crew's members, each rider's bike, outfit, build and lean, and 17,000 triangles for the five.
- **Bike picker.** The rider card cycles the focused rider's bikes (arrows, or up and down). `TourSystem.selectBike` keeps the choice per rider in `localStorage` (`twistyBikes`), and `Game.changeShowcaseBike` rebuilds that rider's stage bike. The card shows `BIKE_MODELS` names and years, never a maker's.
- **Outfits.** A character's `outfits` (`OUTFITS` in `js/vehicle.js`) are what its rider can wear: each has an `id`, a `name` that says what the gear looks like, and a `kit`. `Vehicle.outfitOf` gives the one named in the vehicle's `options.outfit`, else the first; `character.kit` is the first outfit's. `TourSystem.selectOutfit` keeps the choice per rider in `localStorage` (`twistyOutfits`), `getSelectedOutfit` reads it, and `Game.selectedOutfit` hands it to the vehicle. No screen offers the choice yet. A kit's styles pick the cut of each part of the modelled rider (`<part>_<style>` in `rider.glb`), which goes into the rider group under the part's plain name. A character's `build` scales the torso up from the hip and widens the shoulders, the hips and the limbs. The procedural stand-in draws the kit's main colours only. The light rider (`assets/bikes/rider_far.glb`) holds every part and every cut again under the same names at a fifth of the triangles (a rider as worn is 1,010 to 1,220, at most 1,300), for the crew and for a stand-in; `tools/blender/far.py` makes it from `rider.glb`, so a new cut gets its light twin with no code. The ridden rider has no far level: it is never 30 m from its own camera. Every rider has two outfits or more. A new garment is a node `<part>_<style>` in `tools/blender/rider.py` and a style and colours in a kit, with no code for one rider; `backStyle` adds a part to the back of the jacket (`back_<style>`), `backpackColor` the backpack (no rider wears one now). The first outfit of each rider carries a mark that reads from behind, and no two first outfits look alike from there: `tests/unit/characters.test.js` measures the back view of the modelled rider from the kit's colours (60 cm2 of mark, 180 cm2 apart; the rules are in `tools/blender/README.md`). `tests/unit/bikemodels.test.js` rides every outfit against the luggage.
- **Scarf.** A kit's `scarfColor` and `scarfBikes` give the rider a scarf (`Vehicle.buildScarf`) on those bikes only: Guy on the scrambler, in his first outfit (two of the others have a neck tube in its place, and the rain oversuit has neither).
- **Rider select.** The dimmed tier works from each material's lit colour (`userData.lit`), so it can run again after a late swap. The procedural bike darkens a material once for each part that uses it, as before. A modelled bike has few parts, so its palette materials get 0.03 and its other materials get 0.12. Set the tier through `vehicle.onModel` if you add another.
- **Off switch.** `node tools/terrain-check.mjs <probe> --no-assets` keeps the procedural bike and the procedural scenery, for a before and after measure.

## Traffic cars

`Car.createCarModel` (`js/traffic.js`) builds the procedural car, then calls `CarModels.dress` (`js/carmodels.js`). That swaps in a Blender-made model from `assets/cars/` (built by `tools/blender/car_*.py`; see `tools/blender/README.md`). Jest loads `traffic.js` without `carmodels.js`, so the other tests see the procedural car.

- **Bodies.** There are two: a hatchback and a van. The colour of the car selects the body (`CarModels.VANS` holds the four van colours of `Traffic.getRandomCarColor`). No random number is drawn for it.
- **When.** The files start to load when the page opens. If the file is in when a car is built, the swap is immediate. If it arrives later, the swap happens then, but only if `update()` has not run for that car. A car that is on the road keeps its procedural model until it goes off the end of the leg: `Car.update` hides it there and runs `car.modelWaiting`. A file that fails to load, or a model that lacks a part, leaves the procedural car.
- **Contract.** The swap sets the same fields on the car: `body`, `wheels` (four groups on the same hubs, which `update` spins) and the four indicators, each with its own material. `body.material` is the material of the procedural body, so the collision flash still reaches the paint. The headlights (`0xffffee`, 0.8) and the tail lights (`0xff0000`, 0.3) glow as on the procedural car. The rear indicators are the brake lamps. `car.modelled` is true after a swap and `car.kind` names the body.
- **Size.** The model stands on y = 0 and the car's origin is at hub height, so the model hangs 0.32 below the group. Both bodies are 4.3 m long and 2.0 m wide across the mirrors; the procedural car is 4.4 m and 2.0 m. The hatchback is 0.18 m lower than the procedural car, which rode 0.38 m off the ground. The van is 0.14 m higher. `Car.checkCollision` is a ball of 2.2 m about the origin and does not read the model.
- **Random numbers.** three takes each object's id from `Math.random`. `CarModels` gives three a generator of its own while a swap runs. Thus a swap does not move the numbers that `Traffic` draws next, with a seeded generator or at any arrival time.
- **Cost.** A modelled car is about 4700 triangles in 28 meshes; the procedural car is 2238 triangles in 57 meshes. All cars of one body share geometry and all finishes but the paint, the roof and the indicators.
- **Off switch.** `--no-assets` keeps the procedural cars too.

## World road surface

`js/world/roadsurface.js` (`RoadSurface`) makes the world road's textures at load from seeded noise: chip-sealed asphalt (1.5 m tile) and the Via Tremola's granite setts in segmental arcs (1.6 m tile, 10 cm setts, 1.6 m chord, 0.3 m rise). Each is an sRGB albedo with roughness in alpha and a linear normal map (asphalt height or a per-sett number in alpha): four 512 px RGBA8 `DataTexture`s with mipmaps and anisotropy 8, about 5.6 MB on the GPU. `node tools/world/road-textures.mjs <dir>` writes them out as PNG files to look at.

- **Near ribbon (LOD0).** `RoadMesh.nearRibbon` lays seven vertices a row (verge, edge, three across, edge, verge). Its UV is metres across the road and metres along it, restarted at a multiple of `RoadSurface.PERIOD_M` (72 m, which every pattern period divides) so patterns meet across section joints and texture coordinates stay small; `roadBase` carries the restart. Attributes `road` (half width, setts, centre style, hard verge) and `edge` (left and right edge style, left and right side-road mouth) drive the shader: markings, wheel tracks, patches and sealed cracks are drawn from the road coordinates and follow the curve. Lines are box-filtered with `fwidth`, so they fade to their mean far off instead of flickering.
- **Texel density in a bend.** A column `o` m across is `1 - o * curvature` as long as the centre, so the centre's metres would squash the inside of a 10 m hairpin to half. The asphalt's grain has no direction and reads `roadPlane` (ground x, z from an origin on the 72 m grid, so sections still meet). Each 1.6 m arc of setts reads `RoadSurface.settAlong`: the centre's metres less the arc's middle offset times the heading, so its setts keep their size, and neighbouring arcs meet out of step at the joint between them. Markings, patches and cracks keep the centre's metres; a broken edge line is spaced along the edge (`s - lineC * heading`). In a bend a quad is a trapezoid, so `roadArc` carries the metres along and the heading times `q = 1 - o * curvature` and the shader divides by `q`: a line across the road stays straight across the quad instead of kinking at its diagonal.
- **Fading by footprint.** `RoadSurface.footprint` is the metres of texture a pixel covers once filtered: the short axis of its two screen-space gradients, or the long axis over half the anisotropy (4 taps), whichever is more. The minimum of the two widths stays small edge-on, which let the setts' joints beat against the pixels on the far switchbacks. The setts' relief goes flat from 6 mm to 3 cm a pixel, their colour and roughness go to the means from 2 to 6 cm; the asphalt's grain from 4 to 16 mm. The normal map weakens where the road is seen edge-on, and so does the wheel tracks' polish: edge-on their low roughness reflects the sky at near full Fresnel and drew bright stripes along a road seen from below. Each sett is toned again per copy of the tile by the copy of its own row's arc (`RoadSurface.settCopy`), so a sett cut by the tile's edge is one tone; the textures tile with no seam, so the GPU's box-filtered mips tile too. The verge (gravel to grass) uses alpha-to-coverage on the 4x MSAA scene target, so the material writes its own alpha (`output_fragment` replaced).
- **Far ribbon (LOD1) and side roads.** One flat strip in a Standard material with the textures' mean colour, so the change of level does not show.
- **Wet road.** `RoadSurface.setWet(w)` (from `WorldEnvironment.writeRoadSurface`; rain wets the road by its intensity, blended with the atmosphere) sets the near shader's `roadWet` and the far material. At 1 the carriageway keeps half its albedo (`RoadSurface.WET`), its roughness goes to 0.15 (setts 0.30) and the floor of the roughness clamp to 0.08, first in the wheel tracks and the low places; paint darkens less. The verge stays dry. The far material goes to roughness 0.25 and half its albedo.
- **Markings.** Swiss rules (SSV Art. 72 to 74; SN 640 850a as in canton Lucerne's Richtlinie Markierung 653.201): all white, 15 cm; edge line where the real carriageway is at least 5.20 m; broken centre line 3 m / 6 m where it is at least 6.00 m; a continuous centre line in bends under 80 m radius with a 4 m / 2 m warning line for 60 m either side; edge line broken 0.5 m / 0.5 m across a side road's mouth. The ride is drawn 10 m wide, so `RoadSurface.styles` reads the real width from the swissTLM3D class (`RoadSurface.MARKINGS.classWidthM`): the old Tremola road (`4m Strasse`) and the setts carry no paint. `WorldEnvironment.roadStyles` computes them once for the whole ride and adds the junction mouths.
- **Not done.** No low concrete parapet on the old road (the bridge parapets are the only ones). Where the land below a road's downhill edge drops away, the road's underside can be seen from below (seen at s = 7983 looking up at the next switchback, 17 to 75 m away and 5 m up).
