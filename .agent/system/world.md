# World

Every leg has a terrain profile: what lies on each side of the road (a wall, a fall, or run-off ground) and what lies under it. A side is described outward from the centre line, so the code never assumes which side is the valley. Positive lateral is what a profile calls the right of the road (the rider's left on screen: see "Sides and traffic"); which ground is there is the profile's choice.

| Leg | Profile | Left | Right | Board |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mountain-dawn | a course of six parts (see "Long legs") | meadow, wood, valley, village, cliff wall, snow ridge | meadow and river, wood and bridge, valley, village, drop to the lake, snow ridge | `mountain-dawn-v3` |
| 2 valley-run | `cliffLakeMirrored` | drop to the lake | wall | `valley-run-v2` |
| 3 coastal-descent | `coast` | wall | sea cliff, then beach | `coastal-descent-v2` |
| 4 foggy-gorge | `canyon` | wall; drop after the second tunnel | wall; drop between the tunnels | `foggy-gorge-v2` |
| 5 high-pass | `cliffLake` | wall | drop to the lake | `high-pass` |
| 6 storm-valley | `forest` | run-off forest | run-off forest | `storm-valley-v2` |
| 7 night-ride | `forestNight` | run-off forest | run-off forest | `night-ride-v2` |
| 8 winter-pass | `ridge` | snowfield, drop | snowfield, drop | `winter-pass-v2` |

Left and right in this table, in the terrain profiles and in the legs' traffic lanes are the code's sides (the world's are the rider's: "Sides and traffic"): positive lateral `(cos h, -sin h)`. A chase camera looks along the heading, so the code's right is on the left of the screen. On screen high-pass has its wall on the right and the lake on the left; valley-run (mirrored) has its wall on the left and the lake on the right. The vehicle's crash lines (`Hit the left wall`) use the code's sides too.

High-pass is the one cliff leg bound to the frozen golden (see "Golden bindings").

## One terrain model

`js/terrain.js` is the single source of the ground beside the road. It is pure: no THREE, no DOM, no `Math.random`. It loads before `vehicle.js` and `tours.js`; without it every `Environment` constructor throws `Terrain is not defined`, the menu preview included.

A leg has a profile (`Terrain.forLeg(legIndex)`, held as `environment.terrain`). A profile has a `floor`, an `elevation(i)` for the road height and a `left` and a `right` side. High-pass uses `cliffLake`: a wall on the left, a drop to the lake (y = -200) on the right. Valley-run uses `cliffLakeMirrored`: the same two sides swapped, so the drop is on the left and the wall on the right. Mountain-dawn uses `valleyFloor`, storm-valley uses `forest` (see "Run-off legs"), coastal-descent uses `coast` and winter-pass uses `ridge` (see "Coast and ridge"), foggy-gorge uses `canyon` and night-ride uses `forestNight` (see "Gorge and night forest"). Distance `d` on a side is always outward from the centre line, so a side does not know which way it faces.

Three things must agree: the rendered ribbon, `Environment.groundYAt()` and the bike's ground height. All three read the profile: the vehicle and the rockfalls call `Terrain.groundY()` (the vehicle with `exact = false`, the rockfalls with `exact = true`) and clamp or settle on what it returns.

- `Terrain.row(side, j, ctx, out)` gives one cross-section vertex. `Environment.buildTerrainRibbon(sign, side)` draws the mesh from it, one `this.rand()` per vertex.
- `Environment.decorateRockFace(sign, side, group, rockMaterials)` adds rocks, cracks, seepage, vines and snow. It runs only for a side with a `face`. `side.face.rockSpread` says how far rocks spread outward per unit of random height.
- `Terrain.groundY()` finds the nearest centre-line point and calls `Terrain.heightAt()`. An exact read (`exact = true`) is the drawn ribbon itself: every vertex of a ribbon column lies on that column's line, so the point falls in the strip between two column lines, and `meshY` reads the highest of that strip's triangles (float32-rounded, as the mesh stores them). On the road and a ledge an exact read is the road height interpolated along the segment. So scenery and rockfalls rest on what is drawn, on bends and between columns too, on rock, ground and span blends alike. The one exception is `cliffLake` (`columnWalk: true`): high-pass is bound to the frozen golden, which recorded the older read (walk the nearest vertex column with the lateral from the nearest centre-line point, which is up to 1 m off between points on a tight bend). A read with `exact = false` (the vehicle) uses each side's smooth `base()` slope.
- Each side has `bands`: `to` is the distance past the road edge where a band ends, the last band runs outward. `Terrain.zoneAt()` names the zone (`road`, `shoulder`, `wall`, `fall` or `runoff`) and where it starts. The vehicle crashes into a `wall`, falls off a `fall`, and is dragged by `runoff`; rockfalls come off the sides that end in a `wall` at their segment (`Terrain.wallSignsAt()`).
- Dressing follows the side, not a fixed sign. `Terrain.fallSignAt()` is the side that drops (right first): the slope forest of the cliff legs, the roadside bushes and the start sign go there. `Terrain.clutterSignAt()` is where the roadside rock clusters go: the left, unless the left drops. `decorateRockFace()` reads the face in force at each segment, so a side that changes from a wall to a drop is dressed as each.
- `Terrain.facePoint()` is the only way to find a point on a drawn face; the cliff decals (cracks, seepage, vines, snow) are placed with it and tagged `userData.decal`.
- `cliffLake`: flat to `roadWidth / 2 + 2`; the right side then drops 1.33 per lateral unit; the left side is the drawn wall, up to +40 times its height multiplier. `cliffLakeMirrored` is the same on the other sides. The menu preview camera of valley-run stands over the lake (`preview.side: -1`).

If you change a shape, change it in `js/terrain.js` only. Never add randomness to the ribbon vertex positions: scenery would float again. A change to row maths moves every hash in `tests/golden/`; see `tests/README.md`.

A wall is modelled exactly (declared delta D6): scenery that rests on it with `groundYAt()` sits on the drawn rock. The ground model is flat to `roadWidth / 2 + 2` while the wall starts 1 m past the road edge, so a rock at the foot of the wall, inside that ledge, stays at road height. Only the physics bands (`wall` 1 m past the edge) say where a bike meets the rock.

No sign, post, chevron, marker or gate pole reaches into a wall band (declared delta S1, `tests/unit/signs.test.js`). `Environment.clearOfWall(index, sign, want, extent)` gives how far past the edge something reaching `extent` across the road's line can stand, pulled in from `want` to clear the wall band at its segment and the segment either side by 5 cm. Delineator and curve posts stand 1 m past the edge, 0.8 beside a wall; chevrons 1 m, 0.5 beside a wall. A warning board (hairpin or roadworks) is 4 m across: it stands 1.2 m past the left edge, past the right edge where the left is a wall, pulled in over the shoulder where both are (the night village), and is not built where it cannot clear a wall at all. The finish gantry's poles stand 1.5 m past each edge, 0.8 where either side is a wall. The start sign turns side-on to the road where its board cannot face along it (the gorge). Gate limit posts stand half a metre inside the rideable limit. Everything the test checks carries `userData.sign`.

The roadworks plant (bulldozer, barrier, work truck) is not built beside a wall: on rock it would hang on the face, out of reach, and it has no obstacle entry. The two shipping containers on the road and the hairpin barriers (whose lateral is unchanged, only their height follows the wall) remain.

## Rocks and bushes on the drawn surface

A rock, a rock cluster, an outcrop and a roadside bush are seated on the drawn ribbon, not on the smooth `base()`. `tests/unit/scenery-seating.test.js` holds the rules on every leg but high-pass.

- `Terrain.crossings()` lists every drawn surface a vertical line meets (a wall folds, so there can be several). `Terrain.surfaceAt()` picks the one scenery rests on and gives its normal; `least` is the steepest of the surfaces with air above them. `Terrain.clearance()` says how far a point stands clear of the drawn ground (0 inside it). The drawn columns are kept per road and profile, so these reads are cheap.
- `Environment.seatRock()` places a rock. On ground gentler than 35 degrees the rock rests with its centre on the surface. On a steeper face the rock turns its thin axis to the face normal and sinks along it: 0.68 of its thickness at 35 degrees, 0.88 from 55 degrees, so the cap left out is a third of the thickness at most. A rock in a face is also cut down to reach 1.6 m from its centre. A wide boulder covers the wall's facets and reads as stuck on at any depth.
- A bench of a wall counts as the face (`face` argument): a rock past the wall's ledge, a rock on a fold over the ledge that is off the road's height, and every rock of a cluster beside a rock face. These sink and shrink as on a steep face.
- A seat is then measured. At most 15% of the rock's bulk may stand clear of a face (60% of gentle ground), and its lowest point at most 0.3 m over the ground. A rock that fails sinks deeper once, then is left out. Its `rand()` draws are taken in both cases.
- A rock of a face is an outcrop of it (`Environment.outcropLook()`). It uses the face's texture and one vertex colour: the ribbon's colour at its seat (`faceColor()`, the four nearest vertices), times a shade of 0.94 to 1.04. The draw that picked one of the old grey and sand materials sets the shade, so the draw count is the same. Road debris takes the colour of the wall's foot.
- A rock cluster stands only on ground gentler than 35 degrees. A roadside bush roots only in ground gentler than 40 degrees.
- A modelled rock (`js/sceneassets.js`) has its own shape. `Environment.holdRock()` sinks it further until the same measure passes, backs it out when none of it shows, and leaves it out when neither works. The gorge's modelled rocks keep their one tint (`rockTint`, the stone of the wall's detail: see `tools/blender/README.md`, "Rock face detail"), not the face colour at each seat.
- High-pass (`columnWalk`) keeps the old placement: the frozen golden recorded each rock. Its rocks still stand half out of the wall, in the old grey and sand materials.

## Trees and bushes

`Environment.createForest` plants every tree from the seeded generator: a position, a turn, a size, a tint and a kind (conifer or broadleaf), in two instanced meshes named `forest-conifer` and `forest-broadleaf`. Those procedural meshes are what a leg starts with, what jest sees, and what stays if a file fails.

`js/treemodels.js` then redraws them with the Blender-made shapes of `assets/trees/` (`tools/blender/tree_*.py`: see `tools/blender/README.md`, "Trees and bushes"). `SceneAssets.dress()` calls it, so `environment.dressing` resolves when the trees are in.

- Placement does not change. Each instance keeps its matrix and its tint and moves to the instanced mesh of one of three shapes of its kind, picked by a hash of its position. The solid trunks in `environment.boulders` (radius 0.6) are not touched. `tests/unit/treemodels.test.js` holds this.
- A leg draws its trees in six calls (three on winter-pass, conifers only), where the procedural forest takes two. Each shape is at most 220 triangles.
- Winter-pass uses the `_snow` shapes. The snow is in the vertex colours.
- A roadside bush (`addEnvironmentalDetails`) keeps its mesh, its seat and its scale. It takes one of two modelled shapes and a turn about its own centre from the same hash.
- High-pass keeps its procedural trees and bushes (`TreeModels.KEPT`). The frozen golden binds its mesh count, its triangle count and each mesh's vertices (see "Golden bindings").
- No other builder makes a tree. The cone trees of `createDetailedHill` are never built: nothing calls it. The menu preview builds an `Environment`, so it gets the same forest.

A world does not use `js/treemodels.js`. `Scenery` (`js/world/scenery.js`) plants its trees from the land cover and draws each species at three levels of detail from `assets/trees/alpine.glb`. The mid level is the shape a leg draws. See [graphics.md](graphics.md), "The trees' levels of detail".

## Run-off legs

`forest` (leg 6) is built with `Terrain.heightfieldSide`: a list of stations (distance past the road edge, height relative to the road) joined linearly, with an optional rolling undulation along the road. The ribbon rows sit on the stations, so the drawn ground and the physics `base()` agree to 0.10. Both sides are run-off to 25 past the road edge, then a wall. A side must end in an unbounded wall or fall band, so `Terrain.rideableLimit()` is always finite.

`valleyFloor` (leg 1, mountain-dawn) is the journey over to the mountains. It is built with `Terrain.closingSide`: two station lists of the same length, `open` and `closed`, and one weight, `profile.closing(s)`, that moves every station from its open place and height to its closed ones. The weight is a smoothstep over the leg (global segments 0 to 69, `VALLEY_LEG` in `js/terrain.js`, pinned to `js/tours.js` by a test): 0 for the first 30% of the leg, 1 from 80%, smooth between. `Terrain.closingAt(profile, s)` reads it; a profile that keeps one shape answers 0.

| Part of the leg | Ground | Run-off past the road edge | Dressing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open (segments 0 to 20) | Meadow, low hills (about 30 m) 190 m out | 35 each side | Broadleaf, 1 to 3 a side a segment; no rocks |
| Closing (21 to 55) | Hills rise and close in, slopes steepen | Narrows smoothly | Trees thicken and turn to conifers; outcrops start |
| Foothills (56 on) | Valley sides 120 m high, bare rock where steeper than 0.6 | 14 left, 24 right | 3 to 5 a side a segment, 85% conifers; outcrops |

- Each row keeps its station gap at every weight (`splitGaps`), and the ground between two stations is a straight line, so `base()` equals the drawn rows exactly at any `s`. A band may name a `closed` end (`{ to: 35, closed: 14, kind: 'runoff' }`): `zoneAt`, `rideableLimit` and `gateReach` read the band end at their segment, so the run-off, the wall line, solid trunks and gates narrow with the ground. The foot of the left bank moves with the band end, half a metre past it: between two columns the triangles join two places of a moving knee, and the margin keeps that out of the run-off, so the drawn run-off stays within 0.10 of `base()`. The river bed (right, 25 to 40 past the edge) is the same at every weight: the water ribbon is fixed.
- `roll` weights each station's share of a rolling ridge line (`wave`, amplitude 3 open to 16 closed): the run-off stations have none, so only the far slopes roll.
- Limits held by `tests/unit/runoff-legs.test.js` at every weight: rows run outward and reach no further than 200 (half the tightest bend, 444 m at segments 84 to 93), no slope steeper than 1.2 across the road or along it, the run-off no steeper than 0.15, every side ending at least 30 m above the road, and the drawn run-off within 0.10 of the ground the bike rides.
- Road height: 12 m through the open country, climbing about 16 m through the middle. From 14 segments before the leg's end it eases onto `cliffLake.elevation`, and from the end (segment 69) on it is that function, so the finish (segment 74), the 20 built segments past it and leg 2's own start (segment 70) are one road with no step. No step along the whole path exceeds 0.5.
- Dressing: `dressing.forest.closed` holds the tree values at full weight (`coniferShare`, `perSegment`); `dressing.outcrops.perSegment` is the number of rocks a segment at full weight. Outcrops (`createOutcrops`, one instanced mesh named `outcrops`) stand past the rideable limit plus 4, out of the river band, with their centres under the drawn ground so the ground cuts each into a cap. They are not solid: no bike reaches one.
- Backdrop: `dressing.backdrop: ['approach']` builds `createApproachRange`, one merged mesh named `approach-range`: peaks beside leg 2's road (the wall side 300 m out, the lake side 520 m) and a few at the head of the valley, straight on from the leg's end. Each stays clear of the road by its radius plus 120 (the head peaks clear of the road this leg draws). They stand on a base 80 m under the leg's lowest road, behind the valley sides. Fog alone leaves a far peak as a flat silhouette that still shows against the sky, so the material fades the range with view distance: whole within 800 m, gone at 1500 m (`userData.fade`), and a faded fragment is discarded. At 10% of the leg the nearest peak's foot is about 1060 m off (its centre 1400 m) and all but one peak are past 1500 m; at 95% the nearest foot is about 270 m off (centre 660 m). The far ring in the sky rises with the ride too (`render.ringRise`, see `rendering.md`).

- The bike slows and loses grip in run-off, and crashes after 6 s off the road, in water, or on an obstacle. Trunks, rocks and the relocated roadworks plant are `environment.boulders` entries with `solid: true` and a `height`: they are tested while airborne too, by horizontal distance and height, and are never hopped.
- Mountain-dawn has a river ribbon (`floor-river`, 3 m below the road, lateral 35 to 50 on the right). `Terrain.waterLevel()` gives the water height for a lake, sea or river.
- A trunk is solid when it stands within the rideable limit of its own nearest segment (`plantRunoffTrees`), the segment the bike's zone test reads.
- Roadworks plant stands at `-(roadWidth / 2 + 9)` (bulldozer) and `-(roadWidth / 2 + 6)` (barrier, truck) on run-off ground, not at -30 and -25 on a wall.
- A gate (`environment.finishGate`, `environment.checkpoints`) reaches `Terrain.gateReach()` each side at its own segment: the rideable limit plus 2 over run-off, the road's ledge beside a wall or fall. On mountain-dawn that is 47 each side at the first checkpoint and 26 (left) and 36 (right) at the finish. `Game.inGate(gate, position)` is the only gate test; there is no segment window.
- A changed course has `trackVersion` 2 in `Terrain.LEGS`, so `TourSystem.boardId()` gives `mountain-dawn-v2`, `valley-run-v2` and `storm-valley-v2` and local best times use `Game.bestTimeKey()`. `tests/unit/runoff-legs.test.js` pins a digest of each leg's course: change a road height, band, run-off rate or tree layout and it fails until the version is bumped. Progression keeps the plain leg ids.
- The menu preview camera takes its radius and height from `Terrain.LEGS[i].preview`.
- Mountain-dawn's board stays `mountain-dawn-v2` through the closing valley: no v2 board has shipped. Its course digest hashes the closed stations, roll, wave, the weight at every segment and the closed tree values.

## Coast and ridge

`coast` (coastal-descent) has a hill on the left (the cliff wall as on `cliffLake`) and a sea cliff on the right: a fall with a short lip (`lipDepth` 2) onto a sea at absolute height 0 (`floor-sea`, a fall ends at 0.5). The road descends from 58 to 6, so the right side changes along the leg: `profile.spans` replaces it from segment 216 with a run-off beach (sand, 20 past the road edge) that ends in a fall into the sea.

- `profile.spans` is a list of `{ from, to?, left?, right? }` in global segment indices. `Terrain.sideAt(profile, sign, segIndex)` returns the side in force; every consumer (zones, gates, heights, the fall rules) asks it, never `profile.left` or `profile.right`. `from` is inclusive, `to` exclusive and open when absent.
- The ribbon is drawn with `Terrain.rowAt()`, which blends the two sides over one segment (four columns) at each edge of a span, centred on the segment boundary (half a segment before `from`, half a segment before `to`). `Terrain.groundY(exact)` walks the same blended rows. The beach is not exact: `base()` and the mesh agree to 0.10 inside the span, and differ by up to the blend in the half segment before it.
- Rock decoration (embedded rocks, cracks, vines) hangs only where the side has a `face`: `Terrain.faceRange()` bounds it, so the beach gets none. A ribbon that is a face for part of its length keeps the rock material and colours its ground columns by vertex (`side.tint`, else the leg's grass).
- The land side is a drawn wall like the cliff legs': the roadworks plant is not built up it.

`ridge` (winter-pass) has a snowfield 6 m wide each side of the road (run-off, white), then a fall of 40 m (`fallDepth`) onto a shelf, over a frozen lake at -200 (`floor-lake`, `ice` material, `createIceMaterial` in `js/graphics.js`). A fall ends 40 m under the road because the floor is not water. The drop is steeper than the side's `rockSlope` (1.0), so it is drawn as a band of bare rock under the snowfield. The roadworks plant (`LEGS[7].plant`) stands inside the snowfield. The leg has no wall, so no rockfalls.

Winter-pass is dressed as a high pass:

- Peaks (`backdrop: ['peaks']`, `createPassPeaks`, one merged mesh `pass-peaks`): every 5 segments a peak each side, 260 to 560 m out plus half its radius, 220 to 480 m over the lowest road, snow line at 55 to 75% of its height. Each clears the road by its radius plus 60 (`env.passPeaks`). They stand on a base 140 m under the lowest road, so their feet are in the haze below the shelf. `addPassPeak` draws each face on its own with one colour: crests and gullies round the flanks, snow only on a face gentle enough to hold it (above the snow line anything not sheer, round it steeper faces the higher, below it only near-level ledges), dark bare rock elsewhere, each face's shade varied. The peaks take half the scene fog's density (`PASS_PEAK_FOG`, `userData.haze`): at full strength the snow haze left even the nearest a pale silhouette. The depth pass's height fog, and with it the road, is unchanged.
- The snowfield is near white (`tint` 0xf4f6f8; ground colours are stored undecoded, so the old pale blue rendered saturated), and the snow weather leaves the verge strip its own snow colour instead of a worn-dirt blend.
- Conifers: snow-laden (white upper tier slopes in the geometry's colours), 2 to 4 a side a segment, only on the slopes 12 m or more from the road edge, none solid.
- Boulders (`outcrops` with `everywhere: true, snowy: true`): 3 a segment at full weight the whole leg, past the rideable limit plus 4 (on the rock band and the shelf), pale with snow. Not solid.
- `dressing.pass` (`createPassMarkers`): a red, white and black snow pole each side of every segment, 3.5 m past the road edge (instanced `snow-poles`, sunk 0.3 m, not solid); a stone cairn every 12 segments on alternate sides, 5 m past the edge (instanced `cairns`, each a solid circle); and a summit marker (`summit-marker`, two solid posts) on the right-hand snowfield at the highest road point at least 4 segments short of the finish (`env.summitSegment`). None stands in a roadworks zone or the segment either side of one. Only a leg with `pass` builds them, so no other leg's `rand()` draws move.

Both legs have `trackVersion` 2: `coastal-descent-v2` and `winter-pass-v2`. High-pass keeps its heights and its plain board id.

A bike at rest after a fall counts as landed (`hitGround`) when its ground speed is under 0.5; a resting bike's vertical velocity never falls under 0.5.

## Gorge and night forest

`canyon` (foggy-gorge, segments 234 to 321) runs in five sections:

| Segments | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|
| 234 to 264 | wall | wall |
| 265 to 271 | tunnel | tunnel |
| 272 to 285 | wall | drop to the gorge lake |
| 286 to 293 | tunnel | tunnel |
| 294 on | drop to the gorge lake | wall |

A wall is 1 m past the road edge, 57 m high with its height swinging 18% along the road (so 46 to 67 m; `variation` and `snow: false` on the face). A drop is the cliff legs' drop (`cliffDropSide`, a 2 m ledge then 1.33 down per metre) onto the gorge's lake (`floor-lake`, absolute y -28, a fall ends at -27.5). Both tunnels lie on straights and keep a segment clear of every checkpoint (263, 295) and of the roadworks (281 to 283). A tunnel cuts through a rock spur: its sides climb from the lining to 40 m beside the walls, then run down into the lake and end 66 m under the road, so seen from a cliff road the spur is a ridge into the water, not a fin with an edge against the sky. The spur is drawn as bare rock (`rock: true` on a `heightfieldSide`: rock colour and texture, no face to dress), and its cap is rock (`span.cap`). Each change of side blends over one segment (`Terrain.rowAt()` blends from the side of one segment to the side of the next, span to span included). No trees, no mountain backdrop, and no roadworks plant (a wall or drop side builds none). Rocks fall at rate 0.5 from the walls in force at the target segment: both in the canyon (one draw picks the wall), the one wall on a cliff road, none in or beside a tunnel.

`forestNight` (night-ride) is the forest of storm-valley with less roll, a climbing road and two spans, and no rockfalls. Its ground (and the set pieces' sides) reaches 115 past the road edge, because the leg's tightest bend (266.7 m) allows no row past half its radius; storm-valley's reaches 130, because it draws night-ride's first bend (291 m).

A span with a `type` is a set piece: `Terrain.setPieces(profile)` lists them as inclusive segment ranges (`environment.setPieces`), and the world build keeps forest, rock dressing, bushes, delineator posts and rockfalls out of them (rockfalls out of the range widened by one segment each side).

A roadworks zone keeps everything it builds (the board 5 segments ahead of a major zone, 3 ahead of a minor one, and the cones a segment past each end; `Environment.roadworksReach()`) off every set piece and the segment either side (`ROADWORKS_SET_PIECE_MARGIN`), and keeps a checkpoint (`Environment.checkpointSegments()`) out of the zone itself. `roadworksStartClear()` moves a zone that breaks either rule to the nearest start that keeps both, the later on a tie. Only night-ride's minor zone moves: it was segments 592 to 593, with its board in the village, and is now 597 to 598. The gorge's zones and every leg without a set piece keep their places.

| Piece | Leg | Segments | Terrain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tunnel | foggy-gorge | 265 to 271 | Wall 1 m past the edge, `ceiling` 7.5, rock over the road |
| Tunnel | foggy-gorge | 286 to 293 | As above |
| Tunnel | night-ride | 514 to 521 | Wall 1 m past the edge, `ceiling` 7.5, hill over the road |
| Village | night-ride | 583 to 590 | Level pavement (run-off, so a rider is slowed, not crashed) to 3 m past the edge, then house fronts (wall) |

`Environment.createSetPieces()` builds each as a group named for its type, with a few merged meshes standing at the piece's centre (culling reads a mesh origin): the tunnel a lining, hill cap, two portals and an emissive lamp strip (each tunnel reads its own span's `ceiling` and `cap`); the village houses, windows, lamp posts, lamp heads and light pools (see "The modelled village" for what replaces the first four). No piece adds a light: the scene keeps its seven. `Game.tunnelRoofY()` gives the roof over the bike (a segment either side counts) and `updateCamera` keeps the camera 1 m under it. Both legs have a `preview` for the menu camera. A span `type` must be `tunnel` or `village`: those are the only builders.

### The modelled village

The village is built procedurally first (boxes for houses), and that is what jest sees and what stays if a file fails. `SceneAssets.dress()` then swaps it for the Blender-made one (`assets/village/houses.glb` and `props.glb`, built by `tools/blender/village_houses.py` and `village_props.py`). `buildVillage` tags the four meshes to replace with `userData.dressing = { kind: 'village' }` and records the plots in `group.userData.village` (`plots`, `front`, `lampLine`); the light pools are not tagged and stay.

- **Places.** Each plot (a segment and a side) gets one of five houses, its front on the wall line (`roadWidth / 2 + 3`), and a lamp post on the old posts' line. The swap reads no `this.rand()`: which house, its wash, its shutters and which windows are lit come from a hash of the segment and the side. Two neighbours, and two houses that face each other, are never the same house.
- **Garden walls.** The ground behind the fronts climbs 7 m at once (`streetSide` in `js/terrain.js`), and a plot is 20 m long where a house is 11 to 15 m. The gap from one house to the next is filled with sections of a 7 m retaining wall with the garden's fence and hedge on top; a section is 3 m long, and the sections of a gap are stretched or squeezed alike to fill it (0.89 to 1.21 of their length between these houses). The outer flank of the first and the last house has none: the bank tapers away there.
- **Meshes.** Everything opaque is one mesh and everything that shines is another, so the village is 3 meshes with the pools (5 before). Both stand at the set piece's centre. They are about 84000 triangles (3264 before).
- **Light.** No light is added. Lit glass and lamp glass are a `MeshBasicMaterial` whose colours are a little over the night's bloom threshold (1.8). The lamplight on the walls is baked: each vertex of the opaque mesh carries, in the alpha of its colour, what the lamps near it would give, and the material's emissive term is multiplied by it in the shader.
- **Colours.** No texture: every board, stone and roof slab is a face with its own shade. The opaque mesh's colours are linear and stored as 16-bit shares, not floats. `Graphics.fixColorSpaces()` decodes every float colour attribute as sRGB before the mesh's first draw (`Graphics.dressAdded`; `.agent/system/rendering.md`).
- **Cost.** The merge runs on the main thread when the files arrive, once a build: 45 to 120 ms on an Apple M5. A frame in the village costs about 0.3 ms more than the procedural one (`node tools/village-shots.mjs <dir> <label> --gpu --times`).
- **Instanced, by day (Mountain Dawn).** A converted leg names the files in its course with `instanced: true` (`js/course.js`). Merged, Mountain Dawn's 44 plots would be about 230000 triangles in 18 MB of vertices, over a window's 12 MB. `dressVillageInstanced` lays the same houses, washes, walls and lamp posts (`villageLayout`, shared with the merged village, which it does not change) and draws each model once as instances: one `InstancedMesh` per model, tint kind (plaster takes the wash, shutters the paint, the rest neither, as instance colours) and handedness (a mirrored house draws its back faces). That is 35 meshes, about 235000 triangles drawn and 1.5 MB held. Its glass is dark and its lanterns unlit, with no baked lamplight: the leg rides it at golden hour. The same files give the meadow's farmstead its house (`house_farm`) and barn (`house_barn`) at the boxes' places, front to the road (`dressFarmstead`); the shed stays a box. Without the files both stay procedural. `tests/unit/villagemodels.test.js` holds this.

Both legs have `trackVersion` 2: `foggy-gorge-v2` and `night-ride-v2`. High-pass is the only leg with a plain board id.

## Long legs: courses, parts and sections

A converted leg has an entry in `Course.LEGS` (js/course.js) and its own road: local segments `0..segments - 1` and a 30-segment straight tail. `Terrain.adopt` makes it the leg's table row (`trackVersion` + 1, so a new board). Every other leg still cuts its range from the shared course, byte for byte as before; the pins of `tests/unit/course-path.test.js`, `scene-pin.test.js`, `cones.test.js`, `traffic.test.js` and the `scene` probe hold the legs not yet converted. A conversion removes its own leg's pins.

A course is a list of parts (`Terrain.partsProfile`): each part has its own ground (sides), floor, dressing, look, time of day, weather and one landmark. Neighbouring heightfield parts `morph` over 10 to 30 segments; other joins `cut` on a straight. `Terrain.validateCourse` holds the 12 rules.

### Sections and the drawn window

- A converted leg is built in sections of 25 segments (`SECTION`, `env.sections`); the last also builds the run-off. What is built once (`env.fixed`): the path, the plane floors, the start and finish, the gates, the roadworks, the hairpin signs, the set pieces, the landmarks and part scenery.
- Range builders read their range from `buildRange()`: on a shared-course leg the whole leg as before, on a converted leg the current piece (a section's share of one part). While a step runs, `this.scene` is the step's group and `this.config` is the part's row (`partConfig`). Each step has its own seeded stream (`sectionSeed`), so a section is the same in any build order (`tests/unit/sections.test.js`).
- Ribbons number their columns leg-wide and, on a converted leg, hash their vertex grey (`vertexHash`) and take the grass of the road position (`Terrain.lookAt`), so neighbouring sections share a seam column with equal position, colour and normal. The ground model reads no mesh, so a disposed section reads the same ground.
- The window: one section behind the bike, its own, two ahead; the next one builds hidden. `env.update(segment, 4, lastGateSegment)` runs each frame (`Game.SECTION_BUDGET_MS`); a section first drawn appears one step a frame. Sections below `min(k - 1, last gate's section)` are disposed, so a restart finds its gate's section built (`ensureWindow(..., { sync: true })`).
- A set piece (bridge, village) is built once but drawn only while one of its segments is in a drawn section (`pieceGroups`, `moveWindow`), so the modelled village counts only in the windows that hold it: at most 426000 triangles there.
- No cut edge of the ground shows from the road (`updateFarGround`, one mesh `far-ground` rebuilt as the window moves, about 7700 triangles, 4 to 8 ms a rebuild). A side that ends above the road has a back slope from its last row (`FAR_GROUND.back`: 30 m out and 12 m down, then 330 m out and 420 m down), column for column with the drawn ribbons: without it a side was a sheet that ended in the air, and where the valley jogs (segments 432 to 460) the rider saw its back and its edge as a pale slab. Past the window the ground goes on, coarse, for 150 segments (a column every 5, every row as drawn, the window's last column vertex for vertex, the road in its own colour), so the window's end is no longer a cross-section 1 to 1.5 km ahead; its own end is 4 km or more off, past 99% of every part's fog. The plane floors show for the parts it covers. `tests/unit/course-mountain-dawn.test.js` finds every edge of the drawn ground that one triangle alone uses, at twelve window positions, and allows none more than 5 m over the road ahead of the bike unless its part's fog hides 99% of it.
- At the leg start `Game.prewarmCourse` builds one sample section of every part, shows everything, compiles every shader and puts the samples away. Without it a part's first ground, water or weather compiled its shader mid-ride (up to 1.9 s on Apple M5).
- A converted leg shares one rock texture and one water material (`sharedRockTexture`, `sharedWater`); its face rocks are icosahedra of detail 1, not 3; a part may set its slope forest (`dressing.forest.slope`) and the course its cone spacing (`cones.straightEvery`). These keep every window inside the budgets (800 meshes, 450000 triangles, 1400 instances, 1500 solid boulders, 12 MB).
- A converted leg's roadworks plant follows the run-off rule of the part its zone stands in (`createRoadworks`), and its wall decals (cracks, seepage, vines, snow) lie on the drawn face (`layDecalsOnFace`); upright, as the shared course still places them, they stood off a wall that leans back.
- Cones, cars and the crew follow the window (`Cones.createSectionCones`, `Traffic.followWindow`): a car spawns at the window's far end each `traffic.oncomingEvery` segments; rocks fall only in drawn sections of parts with a rate.

## Only the active leg exists

`roadPath` holds all 744 segments on a shared-course leg (a converted leg: its own `segments + 30`). Geometry is built for `startSegment` to `endSegment + 20` only. Anything placed or spawned outside that range hangs in empty space. Traffic uses `legSegmentRange()`; cones and checkpoints read `startSegment` / `endSegment`.

Shared code never reads `roadPath` or the vehicle's `currentRoadSegment`: it asks `environment.route` (`js/world/route.js`; a leg's is `LegRoute` over `roadPath`) and `environment.ground` (the `Ground` contract in `js/world/route.js`; `LegGround` in `js/world/legroute.js`, over `Terrain`). ## Physics only kills where the rider can see why

A world ride may crash or restart the rider only at something drawn. `WorldRoute.nearestSegment` projects in the ribbon's own frame (each row's columns along its own normal, bilinear between rows), so the physics band is the drawn carriageway and `s` has no step across a sample; the chord's frame put the band up to 0.47 m off the drawn edge and made `s` jump on the inside of the held 6 m hairpins, which the cut rule read as a cut across the ladder ("Off route, back to checkpoint" at km 7.9 with nothing hit). The cut rule accepts any forward step up to `cutFloorM` on the road (and holds road skipped over the land apart: "A cut is not counted"). `tests/unit/world-fairness.test.js` and the GPU probe `world-fairness` (both on `tests/helpers/world-fairness.js`) read the whole slice every metre across the drawn road and verge: a physics band off the drawn one by over 0.1 m, a wall or fall on the drawn road with nothing drawn within 0.5 m, or run-off on the carriageway fails, and careful riders take every hairpin at 5 and 7 m/s with no crash or restart.

The gate arch stands only at a world stage's start and finish. Each checkpoint between is a roadworks site on the straight after its station (`js/world/roadworks.js`, whose header describes the gates, the single lane, the lights, the works and the contact rules; `tests/unit/world-checkpoints.test.js`, `world-roadworks.test.js`, `world-roadworks-layout.test.js`, `world-markers.test.js` and the GPU probes `world-fairness` and `world-clearance` hold it). A checkpoint is a timing line for the split and nothing else: nobody restarts at one ("A restart is in place" below). The rules a new world gets with no code:

- **The timing line is the site's entry.** The station's `s` is where the site begins: the entry gate's boards stand on it and the first stop line is `STOP_M` (3 m) past it, so the split is taken before the rider waits at the light or picks a way through the works. The importer puts a station only where a full site fits ahead (`.agent/system/world-data.md`, "Stations"), so no checkpoint is the bare gates.

- **Length.** A site runs on from its station while the road is straight enough (`CURVE_MAX`), wide enough for the open lane (`OPEN_MIN_M`), clear of a junction, a tunnel and a bridge (and `STRUCTURE_CLEAR_M` short of one ahead: the verge narrows toward a bridge over the row before it, and an exit gate there had no verge to stand on), to a most of its own between `LENGTH_SHORT_M` (110 m) and `LENGTH_MAX_M` (550 m), drawn from the station's id (`Roadworks.mostOf`). Under `LENGTH_MIN_M` a station would have the gates only; the importer's rule leaves none such, and the test names one that is.
- **No empty roadworks** (`Roadworks.problems`, `bare`, `solid`). Every site has at least one group of works and `SOLID_MIN` (3) solid pieces, and no stretch of its closed lane's full width longer than `BARE_MAX_M` (30 m) lies outside every group. The layout holds it for any id and any lane a full site can have: a group that does not fit whole before the lane's end stands as far as the lane goes when the lane would else end bare for longer (the gap before it leaves `TAIL_ROOM_M`), and a site short of solid pieces has pallets stood after a group's strip. `tests/unit/world-checkpoints.test.js` names every checkpoint that fails, with its reason.
- **The works stand in groups, from tables** (`Roadworks.layout`; `tests/unit/world-roadworks-layout.test.js`). No site is named in code: a new world gets its sites from these.
  - `GROUPS`: things that belong together. `strip` is the pieces along the outer strip in order, `extra` the pieces of which a group may have one more, `mid` the ramps of the middle line (a `plate` lies in the landing of the ramp before it), `on` the road surface the group belongs to (`asphalt`, `cobbles`, or none for any; a site is cobbled when most of its closed lane is), `most` how often the group may stand in one site. Shipped: `dig` (fence, digger, spoil heap, a steel plate up the spoil), `surfacing` (sign, roller, heap of chippings, sand heap; asphalt only), `stores` (pallets, pipes, kerb stones, compressor, a plank ramp), `welfare` (site cabin, portable toilet, parked van; once a site, no ramp), `setts` (pallets of setts, kerb stones, sand heap; cobbles only).
  - `PIECES`: each strip kind's length along the road, its centre in from the closed edge (`out`) and, for a solid thing, its half width (`wide`, at least the drawn one). `RAMPS`: the middle line's kinds and their lengths.
  - `MOST`: the most of a kind in one site whatever its length (2 diggers, 2 rollers, 1 compressor, cabin, toilet, van). A group whose piece is at its most stands without it.
  - **Order and spacing are seeded from the site's id** (`Roadworks.random`, a seed of its own; never `Math.random`): the groups in a shuffled order, every group once before any comes again, the first one a group with a ramp; each group either way round, with one of its extras or none; `PIECE_GAP_M` between pieces; `GROUP_GAP_M` of plain closed lane (cones and surface only) between two groups, from the last thing of the one; the first group up to `LEAD_MAX_M` into the lane. A group after the first stands whole, or not at all where the lane then ends bare for `BARE_MAX_M` at the most.
  - **No site has the layout of the site before it, nor of any site before it where its draws give another** (`site.layout`, the groups' names in order): `Roadworks.plan` hands each closed site the layouts of the sites before it and the site draws again, up to `LAYOUT_DRAWS` times; a short lane that draws nothing new takes its first draw that is not its neighbour's. The 6 sites of the shipped stage each have a layout of their own.
  - **The ramps' line is clear of every solid thing from end to end** (`site.rampLat`: `RAMP_LAT_M`, nearer the centre line where the road cannot hold `STRIP_M` of strip beside it; a piece that would still reach the line is left out). A rider who takes one ramp can take the next.
  - **A new piece** is a box-built geometry in `Roadworks.geometries` (under about 150 triangles), a row in `PIECES`, a role in `ROLE`, a name in `NAMES` and its kind in `build`'s list; then it may be named in a group. The sites may not draw more triangles than the stage's budget has room for: the roadworks of the whole shipped stage cost no more than its 12 sites did before the checkpoints were halved (28 844 with every board, face and lamp; the 6 sites draw 15 916; `tests/unit/world-roadworks-layout.test.js`).
- **The tapers keep the road's own surface.** The repair surface (`RoadSurface.setWorks`) covers `Roadworks.repairSpan(site)` only: the lane's full width, `REPAIR_INSET_M` inside each end, which is more than the surface's ragged edge reaches. The bike reads no grip from a site (`Vehicle.syncWorldSurface` reads the route's cobbles only), so the tapers ride as the road does.
- **Gates.** Three boards 2.45 m tall on each verge at each end (`GATE_BAND_M`), their foot frame 0.16 m off the carriageway (`GROUP_OUT_M`). `world-clearance` casts only inside a road's drawn width: its cells at the edge reach half a step past it, and read there the boards counted as over the ride.
- **Stop lines.** Traffic blends to the open lane inside the stop lines (`Roadworks.laneShare`, `BLEND_M`), and the taper closes the lane just after (`TAPER_LAG_M`). So a queue waits in its own lane, and whoever leaves the single lane is back in its own lane beside that queue. Blended before the line, the queue stood in the open lane and met the leaving traffic head-on.
- **Lights.** Green one way, amber, all red until the single lane is empty, then the other way. The all-red time is planned for the lane's length at `CLEAR_SPEED_MS`, so a long site has a long cycle. Each light stands on the verge of the road's own width at its place.
- **The rider is one of the queue** (`Roadworks.riderEntry`): traffic keeps its gap behind a rider who waits at a light, and the lights hold all red while the rider is in the lane. A car in the single lane is not slowed by the legs' spacing rule (`Car.inSingleLane`).
- **Ramps** lie on the road's grade: the leave-the-surface rule and the pitch are measured from the road, not from the level (a flat plate on a climb threw the bike).

## A restart is in place

The owner: "remove the restarting at checkpoints - they can just be used for split timing. the 5 second penalty is enough and less frustrating than going back to a checkpoint", and "for the crash reset, don't even confirm - just move the bike and add 5 seconds".

- **Where** (`WorldRun.restartPlace`, `roadS`). The run keeps the last ride distance at which the bike was on the carriageway: every accepted frame there, and a frame that follows on from it (a rider who turns back along the road). A fall onto a lower leg of a ladder is a step no riding makes, so it does not move it. The place is `WorldPhysics.restartBackM` (6 m) behind that, never before the ride's start. So a crash on the road restarts beside it, and a crash off the road, in water, down a drop or on a slope restarts where the bike left the carriageway. A station is never the place for its own sake; a place just behind a timing line leaves the station passed and a finished stage finished (`restartAt`).
- **Across the road** it is the middle of the rider's own half (traffic's lane where that is nearer the centre line), and the open lane through a roadworks site (`Roadworks.pilotLane`), so never the closed lane or the works. The place only ever moves the rider back, so it is never past a stop line the rider had not passed.
- **What the restart does** (`Game.restartInPlace`, for every reason: a crash, the cut rule ("A cut is not counted" below), off route, wrong way, the C key). The bike stands at the place facing along the ride at a standstill; exactly `Game.RESTART_PENALTY_MS` is added, once; the camera resets (`WorldCamera.reset`), the atmosphere snaps to the place (`snapAtmosphere`), the guide's timers and the cut rule start again; the steer ease is reset and presses made during the fall are dropped, so a held key starts from nothing; cars and riders are moved clear ("Contact"); every knocked thing of the roadworks stands again, and inside a single lane the lights are the rider's (`Roadworks.admit`: green its way, with the traffic moved out of the lane).
- **A crash opens no dialog** (`Game.beginWorldCrash`, `updateWorldCrash`, `Game.WORLD_CRASH`). The fall plays as it is drawn for `mostS` (2.2 s), or from `leastS` (1.5 s) once the bike has lain under `stillMs` for `stillS`; a call-out in the 'bad' tone says the cause and the cost, and stays up for `Game.CALLOUT_MS.crash` (4.4 s, twice the fall, so the rider reads it after the bike is back on the road; the one table `Game.CALLOUT_MS` holds how long every call-out about a problem stays: crash, restart, the guide's warning, collision, bump) ("Rode into Lucy  +5 s": the cause is `Vehicle.crashCause`, what the card showed); then the game restarts the rider by itself. Nothing waits for a key. A second crash called during the fall is the same crash. A crash within `quickS` (1 s) of the last restart counts against the place: from the second such crash running, each moves the next place `backStepM` (20 m) further back, so no rider is held in a loop. A crashed bike nothing announced is begun there too, so none is left lying.
- **On a slope.** The place can be on any grade. A rider who cannot reach the ground on a slope (`footSlopeDeg`) is not tipped there: that rule arms only once the bike has moved off since the restart (`.agent/system/handling.md`, "A rider who cannot reach the ground").
- **Restart leg** is on the pause panel of a world ride (R there, through the confirm box), since no crash card offers it. R while riding restarts nothing (it is read only while a card that offers it is open). The classic legs keep their crash card, its Checkpoint and Restart actions and their restart at the last gate: `showCrashNotification` is shared and hands a world's crash to `beginWorldCrash` in its first line.
- **Debug skip** (N): 40 m before the next station, which is now 40 m before the site's entry, on a rolling start as before.
- `tests/unit/world-run.test.js` holds the place, `tests/unit/restart.test.js` the clock, the call-out, the time, the loop rule and the dialogs, `tests/unit/world-restart.test.js` the real slice (every site, every hairpin, a real bike standing at the place and pulling away), `tests/unit/world-contact.test.js` the crew at a restart; the GPU probe `world-restart` rides it in the page.

## A cut is not counted

The owner: "no, cuts don't count - you can't cheat because you have an offroad bike". The rule is the ride's (`WorldRun.update`, the tuning in `WorldPhysics`), not the bike's: `Vehicle` knows nothing of it, so a later mode with no clock can leave it out. It holds on every bike.

- **Road not ridden.** Two things are:
  - A forward jump of the projection that the bike's speed does not explain (`progressSlack`, `maxBankM`, `cutFloorM`): a line across stacked legs.
  - Road skipped over the land (`skip`). While the bike is on the land (past the drawn verge: `Vehicle.runoff`, handed over as `frame.land`), each frame adds the ride distance the projection gained less the metres the bike rode. It never goes under 0. Beside a straight the two are equal. Inside a bend the road is longer than the line over the land. Inside a hairpin of 6 m radius the ride distance passes about four times as fast as the bike. One stay off the carriageway is held to the most that sum reached (`skipPeak`): more than `cutSkipM` (5 m) is road skipped. Metres ridden on the land after the skip (a loop, a zigzag, the outside of the next bend) do not pay it back, and metres ridden first bank nothing. Only the road pays it back: the peak counts no further than the ride distance the bike is ahead of where it went onto the land (`landFromS`), so a rider who turns back is level. On the land the sum is the whole measure of a forward step. The cost: well outside a bend the projection can stand still and then step 12 m; held to its peak that is a cut (read as it stands it added up to nothing).
- **The verdict waits for the road.** On the land nothing is a cut yet. With road skipped no station is credited and the place to restart from does not move. A rider who turns back to where the bike left the road rejoins free. A rider who rides extra metres on the land does not. Once the bike is on the carriageway with road skipped, and that holds for `cutConfirmMs` (250 ms), it is a cut. Before, a jump was judged 250 ms after it was made, on the land too.
- **What a cut costs.** `Game.updateWorldRun` calls the one restart with the reason `cut`: the rider goes back to where the bike left the carriageway (`roadS`, `restartBackM` behind it), with exactly `Game.RESTART_PENALTY_MS` and the call-out "Cut the road, back where you left it +5 s".
- **What stays free.** Riding beside the road on the land and coming back to it. The drawn verge, on the inside of a hairpin too. A stay off the road that ends on the carriageway with a peak of `cutSkipM` or less is forgotten, so clips do not add up.
- **Why metres and not seconds.** Before, the land's low speed held a cut: no scripted cut beat the best road line by more than 0.15 s, and an off-road bike got its pace 2 s late for that. With a real pace on the land a straighter line wins time with no road skipped at the edge of the measure, so a time bar cannot hold. A variant that gave the bike the road's inner edge as credit let 12 to 15 m chords through (0.5 to 0.95 s gained); the plain metres do not.
- **What the rule cannot see.** A line that keeps to the verge on the inside of a hairpin skips up to 9 m of ride distance and is counted: the verge is drawn as road. The off-route rule is apart from this: more than `Route.OFF_ROUTE_M` (40 m) from the ride for 10 s restarts the rider (`WorldGuide`), which is what bounds how far a timed ride goes over the land and along a side road ("A side road is open").
- Proofs: `tests/unit/world-run.test.js` ("road skipped over the land"), `tests/unit/world-offroad.test.js` (every scripted cut of every hairpin of the shipped stage, on a road bike and on the top off-road bike at 10 and 17 m/s: a counted ride skipped no more than the allowance over the land at its peak, a ride ended as a cut did skip road, every crash has its cause), `tests/unit/world-offroad-map.test.js` (leg to leg over an open slope; every hairpin cut by 30 m each side and then up to 90 m more ridden on the land is still a cut with one restart and one 5 s), the GPU probe `world-cut`.

## No structure stands in a carriageway

One rule for every structure of every road (deck, soffit, pier, parapet, retaining wall, wall end, apron, tunnel tube): it stands in no other road's drawn carriageway, and on the ride's own carriageway nothing of the ride's works stands either. `Carriageways` (`js/world/roadmesh.js`, `env.carriageways`) is the one index the builders ask: the ride in its ribbon frame (`WorldRoute.nearestSegment`) and every side edge whose chunk has been resident (indexed by chunk key, kept after eviction). A structure stands in a road when it rises over that road's surface by more than `LOW_M` (5 cm) and starts under `HEADROOM_M` (4.5 m) over it; a deck whose soffit is 4.5 m over a road passes over it. `guard(owner)` leaves out the owner's own carriageway and the edges it goes on as through nodes of degree 2.

- `RoadMesh.edgeWorks`: an edge point whose wall, parapet or apron would stand in another road moves in over its verge (to `lc`/`rc`) until clear; where nothing clears, the segments either side take no works, so at a junction a side road's walls start where its carriageway leaves the ride's and the run's end turns back under its own road. Each segment is tested every metre between its ends too.
- `RoadMesh.sideStructures`: a row whose deck (soffit to its top, under the road) stands in another road has no deck, and a parapet segment that would stand in one is left out, so at a junction a side road's deck runs on under the road it meets and its parapets stop at that road's edge. `RoadMesh.placePiers` puts a pier every `PIER_EVERY_M` (24 m) and moves one whose box stands in a road or its verge (plus `PIER_CLEAR_M`) along the deck, a metre at a time, to the nearest clear place: a deck crosses a road in one span. A side tunnel's roof stays a deck's depth under any road over it.
- `WorldEnvironment.liftSide`: a side road crossing over the ride between `SIDE_PASS_M` (2 m) and `SIDE_CLEAR_M` over it is lifted to `SIDE_CLEAR_M` (headroom, deck and 0.1 m), ramped over at most 40 m and never past its own edge's ends. The Gotthard road's bridge at km 5.1 crossed 2.5 m over the ride, with a pier on its centre line.
- Works built before a nearby side road's chunk was resident are built again when it is (`chunk:<k>` in `waitingOnTile`, for ride sections and side chunks), so the window's build order does not change them.
- Piers are drawn, not solid: the physics has no pier, so none may stand where a rider can go.
- The exceptions are the ride's own works, which the physics reads whatever is drawn. Its retaining walls keep their place, across a side road's mouth too (`guard.walls`): the physics makes them solid from the importer's drop zone (`WorldGround.onRetainingWall`), so a wall moved off the mouth left the rider stopping at nothing (the Bedrína junction, km 1.27). And its works do not move for another leg of the ride: the physics reads the verge as ground and the wall between two legs as a wall, so a wall pulled in off the lower leg doubled the points where the physics ground left the drawn one (`world-floating`, 357 to 721).

Every deck, parapet and wall is one swept surface: `RoadMesh.rail` sweeps a parapet along a run of edge points with shared corners (no underside, so it shares no plane with the ribbon), and the ride's bridge deck is swept along its rows (`RoadMesh.bridge`), flush with its parapet's outer face (`BRIDGE_DECK_M`). A ride section reads a row of each neighbour (`RoadMesh.section` `head`, `tail`) for the bend and the runs, but draws only its own: before, each section drew the first segment of the next one too, a ribbon segment, bridge and wall end twice in one plane every 256 m. Before, each segment's parapet was a level box, which stepped on every grade (16 m segments on a side road at LOD1). A side road's stub draws its far ribbon from the stub's last row, not under the stub; drawn twice, the two lay in one plane, decks and parapets included. A side road inside the ride's band is held `SIDE_UNDER_M` (5 cm) under it. The ride's far ribbon (LOD1) puts its works at the verge's outer edge, as the near one does.

`tests/unit/world-structures.test.js` (on `tests/helpers/world-structures.js`) streams the slice and fails on a structure triangle in a carriageway (an apron counts from 10 cm over a road: it lies 6 cm under the ribbon's edge and is drawn behind it), a wall or a fall the physics makes on a side road's stub, two corners of one road's structures 2.5 to 25 cm apart (a step or a gap), and two drawn triangles in one plane within 2 cm that overlap (z-fighting).

## Off the road, and at walking pace

Leaving the road is costly, and every cost has something the rider can see (`WorldPhysics`, `Vehicle.worldSurface`, `worldLand`). The bike's surface is read from where it stands against the drawn road, not from the tile's 8 m zone layer, whose road and bench nodes reach several metres onto the land (before, 26 000 of 60 000 points 1 to 24 m past the edge read as the carved bench, with 0.1/s of drag and no off-road clock, and the bike gained speed off the road at full throttle: 10 m/s at the edge, 23 m/s 2 s later).

- The carriageway, or a side road's (`WorldGround.sideRoadY`): road.
- The drawn verge, `RoadSurface.VERGE_M` past the edge: `benchDrag`, nothing else.
- The land past it: drag `landRoll` x speed and `landDrag` x the speed over the cap (at most `landDecelMax`), the throttle keeps `landTraction` and none over `landCapSpeed` (4 m/s: at full throttle on flat grass the bike holds about that), the brake `landBrakeGrip`, the lean `landGrip` (a cap of about 33 degrees, and the lowside reads it as on the road), gravity along the drawn ground's own slope. The drawn bike bobs (`landBumpM`) and rocks (`landWobbleDeg`), and the chase camera shakes. Over `landCrashSpeed` for `landCrashS` crashes ("Too fast on the grass"); land steeper than `landSteepSlope` (0.85, above the 1:1.5 batter beside every road) for `landSteepS`, measured `landProbeM` ahead and across on the drawn ground, crashes ("Rode onto a slope too steep to hold", or over a drop edge). The 10 s off-road timer is gone. The crash card names the cause (`Vehicle.crashCause`).
- The cap is under hairpin speed for a bike with no off-road trait: at 4 m/s it gains nothing over the land. That is not what holds a cut any more: the cut rule does, on every bike ("A cut is not counted" below).
- The land's crashes name what the rider sees. "Too fast on the grass" names the land cover under the bike (`WorldPhysics.landCover`: in the forest, on the scree, on the rock, on the snow). A wall and a drop of the tile's zone layer say "Hit the wall" and "Went over the edge of a drop" (before, the call-out said only "Crashed"). A wall, a parapet or a building touched by the tyres alone, at a glance, is a brush and not a crash: the bike is held at the face, loses speed and rides on, and the call-out says "Brushed the wall" (`WorldPhysics.wallBrush`; `.agent/system/handling.md`, "A brush against a wall"). A drawn building is solid for every bike (`Scenery.houseAt`, the wall rects of the houses the scenery drew, plus `houseHitM`): "Rode into a building". Past the tiles the world has in detail (`WorldGround.mapped`) the bike falls: "Rode off the edge of the mapped land". Trees and boulders are drawn, not solid.
- An off-road bike rides the land by degrees, from one number in its catalogue entry (`BIKE_MODELS` `offroad`, 0 to 1; `Vehicle.landValues`). `.agent/system/handling.md` ("Degrees of off-road capability") has the rule, the tables and the numbers per bike. In short: a bike with no trait reads the world's own values; trait 1 rides most of the map (about 17 m/s on open grass, 12 on scree, 8 in forest, a 3 m bank of about 55 degrees, no crash under 28 m/s); the bikes between follow the trait, and none is under what it had when the trait was one straight line to 6.5 m/s. The pace is the bike's at once: the 2 s wait is gone.
- The power slide (`Vehicle.updateWorldPhysics`, the `slide*` values): an off-road bike on the land with the throttle open steps its rear out where a road bike lowsides. `hold` is the trait x the throttle opening, 0 with the brake over `slideBrake`, off the land or in the air. With a hold the lean limit rises to `slideLean` x hold over the grip, the path turns no tighter than the grip gives, the drawn bike heads `slideAngle` further into the turn (`slideGain` x hold x the demand over `1 - slideOnset x hold` of the grip, at most `slideMaxDeg`; `yawAngle` stays the way the bike travels, so the camera, the cut rule and the progress read the path), the speed scrubs by `slideScrub` x the angle, and the lowside margin rises from 1.15 to 1.15 + `slideRoom` x hold (1.75 on the trail single at full throttle). Past that it falls ("Slid too far: the rear came round on the grass"); with the throttle shut or the brake on hard the lowside is every bike's. The rear wheel is drawn spinning faster (`slideSpin`) and throws dust (`ParticleSystem.createDustCloud`, from `Game`). Nothing of it acts on the carriageway or the verge, or for a bike with no trait.
- On a classic leg the same trait moves the run-off's drag, grip and floor speed toward `LEG_OFFROAD` (`Vehicle.legRunoff`: at trait 1, drag 0.35 to 0.15, grip 0.5 to 0.7, floor speed 6 to 10 m/s). A leg has no grip lowside, so no slide.

The hairpin assist acts only on the carriageway (the recovery pull, which moved a bike past the edge back to it, is gone), and never against the rider's steering unless it turns the bike toward the centre line (`Vehicle.worldAssistRate`): it can hold a full-lock rider off the inside of a hairpin, but it cannot pull a bike outward or resist a rider steering back onto the road. Before, the line capture took up to 1.5 rad/s of lean rate off a rider steering back from the outside of a hairpin.

At walking pace nothing holds the bike up or pushes it along: the lean limit rises to `lowLeanDeg` (40) between `lowLeanSpeed` and `lowLeanFullSpeed`, so the rider lays the bike over for a U-turn (full lock at 3 m/s: 40 degrees, a 2 m radius; before 18 degrees). The creep is gone: with the throttle closed the engine brakes to `idleSpeed`, then the bike rolls down at `rollDecel`. Under `fallSpeed` a bike leaned past `tipLeanDeg` for `tipS` falls over ("Fell over at walking pace"); nearer upright the rider puts a foot down and it stops, the lean easing out.

The steer keys of a world ride are eased before they reach the bike (`SteerEase` in `js/input.js`, `WorldPhysics.steerKeys`). The world's lean is a rate set by the steering, with the self-righting at the square of what is let go (`Vehicle.updateWorldPhysics`), so a rider who tapped a key to hold a line got full rate in, righting out, full rate in: with keys tapped 80 ms on and 80 off, or 120 on and 60 off, the lean rocked by 3.5 to 6.2 degrees and the drawn bar by 3.2 to 4.7.

- Each change of the keys is a straight ramp of the steering: over `keyS` (0.07 s, the 35 ms lag of the smoothing it replaces), or over `featherS` (0.16 s) when the two changes before it each came within `featherGapS` (0.3 s) of the last, which is a rider tapping. Tapped keys then give nearly their mean: the lean rocks by 0.4 to 0.6 degrees and the bar by under 1, and shorter taps hold less lean (after 3 s at 15 to 25 m/s, 50 on and 100 off: 12 to 14 degrees; 80 and 80: 28 to 33; 120 and 60: 48, near the limit).
- A held key, a flick from one side to the other and a release take the short ramp, so they answer as before (to 20 degrees at 20 m/s in 0.363 s, before 0.361; the flick to 40 degrees the other way 1.128 s, before 1.133). The first gap after a long hold is also a short ramp: only from the third change on is the rider known to be tapping.
- A ramp gives the lean a press of its own length gave before, whatever the ramp's length, so a tap still moves the bike by the same amount.
- `InputHandler` moves the ease on at each key event as well as each frame, so a tap is as long as the rider made it and not a whole number of frames. The touch buttons are read once a frame.
- Only the rider's keys and touch buttons go through it (the game has no analogue steering). An autopilot (`tools/autopilot.js`, the probes of `tools/terrain-check.mjs`), the crew and the unit tests steer `Vehicle` directly, so `Vehicle` and every pinned ride are unchanged. The drawn handlebar (`Vehicle.BAR`) is unchanged too: with the lean smooth, the bar is.
- The classic legs keep `steeringSmoothing`: their lean has its own momentum, and tapped keys through either smoothing rock it the same there (measured, not eased).

Proofs: `tests/unit/world-offroad.test.js` (the slice: a road bike against aaa6f8c, and no scripted hairpin cut counted on a road bike or on the top off-road bike), `world-offroad-bikes.test.js` (the trait, its degrees against c111f28, the slide and a bike without it unchanged, on a flat plane), `world-offroad-map.test.js` (the top bike across a real open slope and put back by the cut rule; water, a wall, a drop and a building), `world-edge-return.test.js`, `world-low-speed.test.js`, `world-verge.test.js`, `world-steer-ease.test.js` (the eased keys), and the fairness suite above.

## What stands beside the road stands on the ground

Rules for every world and every kind of roadside thing (`js/world/scenery.js`):

- **Height from its own place.** A thing's height is the drawn ground at its own (x, z): `Scenery.footY` (the verge, the fill beside the road or the land, `WorldGround.groundY`), read beside the ride point it stands by. Never the road's height beside it: the yellow signposts took that, and stood in the air over a bank that falls away, or in the bank where it rises.
- **No ground, no thing.** Where there is no ground to read (the tile is not here, a tunnel or gallery, water) the thing is left out. A place nearer to another strand of the ride or to a side road is left to that road: its fill may lie over this one's.
- **Road furniture stays at the road's level.** A marker stone stands only where the ground at its foot is within `ROADSIDE_STEP_M` of the road (not at the foot of a retaining wall, not under a bridge); else it is left out.
- **What is placed comes from the photos** (`.agent/sop/photo-match.md`). The photos of the area show granite posts along the road's edge, dry tussock grass and low green clumps right beside the road, boulders in the grass, and no yellow sign. So: `stones` (rows of granite blocks at sharp drops, round the outside of hairpins on a kerb course, and on the coping of walls with no parapet: see "Marker stones stand at sharp drops, round hairpins and on wall tops" below) and `tufts` (on the strip within `TUFT_REACH_M` of the ride's verge, which the trees and boulders leave open; one mesh for each tile, no instances, at most `TUFT_CAP`, paid for out of `TRIANGLE_CAP`).
- **Clear of every road.** Each kind has its margin in `Scenery.CLEAR_M`, measured to the whole footprint.

Proofs: `tests/unit/world-clearance.test.js` rides the slice and fails on any tree, boulder, marker stone or tuft whose foot is more than 0.1 m over the drawn ground under it or whose top is under it ("nothing floats"; a planted thing at the road's height beside it is caught), and on any footprint in a road band; `tests/unit/world-meshes.test.js` holds the placement on a synthetic tile; `node tools/terrain-check.mjs world-clearance --world tremola --gpu` casts on the drawn meshes (not on the tufts, one mesh for a tile: the unit test measures each tuft). The window is within about 1 000 triangles of its 450 000 where the land is densest (km 4), so a new roadside kind must say what pays for it: check with `world-budget --every 1000`. A new kind of roadside thing gets a name in `typeOf` (`tests/helpers/world-clearance.js` and the probe) and a margin, and these checks hold it with no other change.

### Marker stones stand at sharp drops, round hairpins and on wall tops

On an old pass road the granite stones are the guard at a sharp drop, a row of them goes round the outside of a hairpin where that outside is a drop, and on a low retaining wall a row of them stands on its top in place of the parapet. They are not at every drop, every such hairpin or every such wall. The rule is read from the ground, the route and the wall plan of any world, with no place named in it (`Scenery.stoneSpot`, `stonePlace`, `stoneStretches`, `hairpinRuns`, `wallRuns`, `stoneSites`; the numbers are one table, `Scenery.STONE_RULE`).

- **A sharp drop.** The ride has a station every `STONE_GAP_M` (2.5 m) from its start, on each side. A station may have a stone where the drawn ground `reachM` (10 m) past the verge lies more than `dropM` (7 m) under the road. The ground is the one the game draws and the physics reads (`WorldGround.groundY`: the land laid back and cut to the walls, and the fill beside the road). A plain fill at the batter falls 6.7 m in 10 m, and the downhill edge of a mountain road stands on one for kilometres: the rule takes only the banks that fall more steeply. With 3.5 m in 6 m, 1 900 stations of the shipped stage would have a stone and the cap would leave most of them out.
- **The outside of a hairpin.** The hairpins are the game's own: `WorldRoute.hairpins()` lists them by the measure of `WorldRoute.kindAt` (a swing of 150 degrees inside 80 m: `WorldRoute.KIND`, the same as `tools/world/verify.mjs findHairpins`). Two bends that turn opposite ways are two hairpins, also when they are less than 80 m apart: the shipped stage has 35 where `findHairpins` has 31 stretches. Each has its arc, `s0` to `s1`: the road on which the swing goes from `arcShare` (0.1) of itself to 0.9 and the road bends (`arcTurn`, 30 degrees, inside `arcSpanM`, 24 m), so a long lead into the bend is not in it. A row stands on the arc, on the side `-sign` (the outside), only over the part of it that is a drop: where the drawn ground `reachM` past the verge lies more than `hairpinDropM` (4 m) under the road, by a stretch's measure. A stretch keeps `dropM` (7 m): a plain fill falls 6.7 m in 10, so at a stretch 7 m takes only the steeper banks, but the outside of a hairpin is where a rider runs wide, and a hairpin on a slope falls 6 to 7 m on its fill leg. With 7 m 1 of 35 hairpins of the shipped stage had a row (km 6.25), with 3 m 10 (one of them under the rising bank at km 1.3, a part of its arc only 3 m over the land below); with 4 m 8 have one (km 3.48, 3.95, 5.88, 6.25, 7.88, 8.90, 9.62, 10.50), 73 stones on 65 pieces of kerb, and the km 1.3 hairpin has none. A hairpin that turns under a bank that rises from its outside, or round level ground or a plain fill, has no row. Its stones are `STONE_GAP_M` apart along the outer edge itself (`Scenery.hairpinRuns`): on the centre line of a bend of 6 m radius they would stand 4.6 m apart. Along a hairpin's arc the drop rule places nothing, on either side, so no stone stands on the inside of a hairpin.
- **On the top of a low wall, in place of its parapet** (`Scenery.wallSpan`, `wallRow`, `wallRuns`, `WorldEnvironment.wallTop`, `wallStoned`; `wallMaxM` 4, `wallShare` 0.6, `wallLowM` 0.3). A low wall is a whole wall under the ride's edge (from its first row to its last, read on across the sections' plans) whose face drops less than `wallMaxM` from the road's edge all along (the face's drop to its foot and into the land drawn in front, `RoadMesh.wallDepth`, the measure the parapet's rule reads), that holds the road up (`wallLowM` or more all along; a cut wall that rises over the road gets none) and that meets no structure (`abuts`: a bridge's parapet ends there). A share of the low walls, drawn from the metre of the wall's first row and its side (`Scenery.wallUsed`), has stones over all of it, `STONE_GAP_M` apart along the middle of its coping, each seated on the coping with no kerb course; a wall where a stone would stand in a carriageway, a stopping place, a roadworks site or a side road's mouth has none at all. On a stoned wall the stones are the guard: `WorldEnvironment.wallStoned` is the one answer, by whole wall, for the stone drawn (`RoadMesh.edgeWorks` draws no parapet on its segments, `plan.stoned`, and keeps its coping `COPING_W_M` deep), the stones (`Scenery.stoneSites`), the physics (`WorldGround.zoneAt`: the zone at the coping is a wall that holds as a parapet does, `parapet`, whatever the land beyond) and the chase camera (`WorldEnvironment.cameraFaces`: the face stands a stone's height, `STONE_HEIGHT_M`, over the coping where the parapet's stood `PARAPET_H_M`). A wall that is not low, or that the share leaves out, keeps its parapet where it drops `PARAPET_DROP_M` (2.5 m) or more (`RoadMesh.railed`). No wall of either world is low today: on the shipped stage the least drop of a wall's face anywhere is 5.8 m (km 2.79) and the lowest whole walls drop 7.3 m (km 9.54) and 8.1 m (km 4.27) at their highest; on the fixture world 7.1 m. At 10 m 3 walls of the shipped stage would qualify and the share would take 2 (km 4.27 and 9.52), at 12 m 5 and 3. `wallMaxM` stays 4 m: the owner's number for a low wall; no stone stands on a wall top on either world.
- **Every wall's top is wide enough for a stone** (`RoadMesh.COPING_W_M`: `Scenery.STONE_WIDTH_M` 0.6 m and `COPING_MARGIN_M` 0.1 m either side, 0.8 m). Every retaining wall, its piece that joins two roads' walls and its face past its end carry a level coping `COPING_UP_M` (`WALL_MIN_UP_M`, 0.5 m) over the road's edge and `COPING_W_M` deep from the face, and the face rises to it. A coping does not fold or overlap on a tight bend (`RoadMesh.copingDepths`): each row has one depth along its own way in, and the two pieces that meet there share it, so they join on one mitre. The depth keeps the coping `COPING_W_M` square to both pieces, to at most `MITRE_MAX` (2) times that at a sharp turn. Where the bend is so tight that a piece's inner edge would run back on itself, the coping narrows at that piece's ends until the edge runs on. It narrows to no less than `COPING_W_M` where a marker stone may stand (no parapet) and to no less than `PARAPET_W_M` where the wall falls `PARAPET_DROP_M` or more. On the shipped slice and the synthetic world no coping needs to narrow. The piece that joins two roads' walls lays its coping along each run's own way at its ends, so it meets that run's coping; where those two ways would twist it across its face, it lays it square to its own line (`RoadMesh.wallJoin`). A side road's wall end that such a piece joins has no face past it and no return, as on the ride (`foot.joined`): a face past it stood a hand from the joining piece, two walls side by side with their copings over each other (km 2.4). The spikes check's `folds` counts every piece of a wall's top folded over itself or over the piece beside it, limit 0 (at 6d78a4e 1, with the 0.8 m copings laid square to each segment 4, now 0 on both worlds). A wall with a parapet has the coping under it. The physics reads the road there, 0.5 m under the coping, and stops the bike at the wall's side to the road (see "Every wall stands at least 0.5 m over the road"); the land is cut 0.3 m behind the face as before, under the coping and the road. A parapet stands on the coping, its face going on from the wall's. The stone material has a fixed depth offset only (`RoadMesh.stoneMaterial`): with one scaled by the slope to the view, the coping seen edge-on from its own height was drawn in front of the wall's face as a line across it. The checks read the wall's top as its coping: `world-walls` measures its top and its open ends from the road's edge, 0.5 m under it, the spikes check's `thin` asks every wall piece but a return for a coping `copingM` (0.8 m) deep, and `world-watertight` reads no foot on a coping (a near-level top of stone) and none of a parapet that stands on one.
- **On a bare edge.** At a drop and round a hairpin: no stone where something else guards the edge: a retaining wall under it (the wall plan, `WorldGround.bridgeOrDrop`; its parapet is the guard, or its top is the wall's own row's) or going on past its end with its coping (`WorldGround.taperAt`: a row whose stretch now runs on to a wall's end stood beside the taper's stone), a bridge, a tunnel or a gallery. None in a stopping place, within `worksM` of a roadworks site (`Scenery.stoneShut`, from the first thing of the site to the last), or within `mouthM` of a side road's band (a junction's mouth). A place nearer to another strand of the ride is left to that strand. At a drop there is also none on a level verge and none on the uphill side.
- **Not at every drop, not at every hairpin; all of one or none.** A stretch is the whole run of stations next to each other where a stone may stand at a drop, read on across the tiles to its ends (`Scenery.stoneStretches`): it ends only where a station is no place for one (no drop, a junction's mouth, a wall, a structure, a stopping place). It is at least `minM` long (4 stones). A `share` (0.6) of the stretches has its stones over all of it, drawn from the stretch's first station and its side (`Scenery.stoneUsed`). Before, the ride was read in 60 m blocks and one drop could have stones on part of it and stop in its middle. A `hairpinShare` (0.75) of the hairpins has its row, drawn from the metre of the hairpin's apex (`Scenery.hairpinUsed`): every run of free places on the arc at a drop, at least `minM` long, or none. All read the ride's own metres, so the order the tiles arrive in does not change them.
- **The stone and its kerb.** A stone is a sawn granite block, `STONE_WIDTH_M` (0.6 m) square and `STONE_HEIGHT_M` (0.68 m) high, square to the road with a few degrees of play. A hairpin's row stands on a kerb course (`Scenery.KERB`: 0.66 m wide, 0.12 m high), a piece from each stone to the next (`Scenery.kerbPart`, instances named `stone-kerbs`). At a drop and on a wall the blocks stand alone, each in the ground or on the coping: the kerb under a drop's row was taken for a wall's top (the owner's picture at km 2.8: the row on the lower road there stands on no wall, its pale strip was the kerb course with its sides down the fill). The kerb is drawn in the dry-stone granite of the retaining walls (`RoadMesh.stoneMaterial`; `LandShade.INSTANCED` reads an instance at its own place in the world). Sizes and colours are measured on the owner's photos and stated as numbers at `Scenery.STONE_COLORS`.
- **Its seat.** A fill starts to fall at the verge's edge, and a stone stands 0.67 m past it: on the fill it stood 0.45 m under the road it guards. So a stone's seat is the verge's edge beside it where that is higher than the ground at its own middle (`stoneSpot` `seat`), and the kerb runs from seat to seat. The stone's root (`STONE_ROOT_M`, 2 m) and the kerb's sides (`KERB.rootM`, 2 m) go down into the ground. Round the outside of a hairpin the fill is drawn flat from one row of the ride to the next, inside the cone the physics reads: 0.67 m past the verge it was drawn up to 1.8 m under the road. There the row stands on a low dry-stone wall. Where the ground falls more than `footM` (0.6 m) under a stone's own foot that stone is left out, and with it a stone that would then stand alone.
- **Which stones are drawn.** A whole pass with its hairpins is in the window at once (the shipped stage had 537 stones, up to 349 of them on the nearest tiles together, when every hairpin's outside had a row; it has 211 now). So the stones stand on the nearest tiles only (LOD 0: `Scenery.setStones` from `WorldEnvironment.buildScenery`; a block is under a pixel from 700 m), and of those the `STONE_CAP` (140) nearest the bike are drawn (`Scenery.rankStones`, read again when the tiles change and for every `STONE_RANK_M`, 20 m, the bike moves). ## Stopping places

A world tells the game where a rider can pull in and wait: `environment.places` (`WorldPlaces`, `js/world/places.js`), from `manifest.places` (the importer's rules and what the map holds are in `world-data.md`, "Stopping places"). No place is named in code. The group ride waits in them (`group.md`): a rider takes a slot with `places.next` and `places.stopAt`.

- **What the game is told.** `places.list` is the open places in order: `{ id, kind, source, s, side, length, depth, taper, surface, name, slots }`. `places.next(s, side)` is the first that starts at or after ride distance `s` (on one side when asked). `places.stopAt(place, slot, ground)` is where the rider in that slot stops: a point clear of the carriageway (1.2 m or more past its edge, in the middle of the strip), facing along the ride, at the ground's own height there. Slot 0 is the far end; each next rider stops 2.6 m behind. `places.at(s, lateral)` is the open place a point is in. `places.all` holds the closed ones too.
- **Two sources, three kinds.** A `map` place is a mapped car park (`carpark`) or rest area (`layby`) along the ride. A `found` place is where the map has none: a `layby` (level verge) or a `viewpoint` (the land falls away past it). The importer decides the kind from the land (`world-data.md`, "Stopping places"); the game reads `kind`. A found `verge` of the first imports (no surface) is still read: nothing is drawn for it.
- **Every place with a surface is drawn and carved** (`WorldPlaces.drawn`): asphalt for a car park and a viewpoint, gravel for a layby (`SURFACE` in `tools/world/places.mjs`). A found place is 2.6 m past the 1.1 m verge (3.7 m past the carriageway's edge), 24 to 32 m long, tapered over 6 m at each end, so it reads as a pull-in and not a notch. A waiting bike stands with its middle 1.85 m past the edge (`stopAt`): its 0.8 m are 1.45 m clear of the carriageway, with as much hard standing behind it.
- **A drawn place is the ride's own verge, wider on one side.** `WorldPlaces.apply` writes the place's pad (its depth, tapered at each end: the importer's `padOf`) on the route's points as `padN` and `padP`. `RoadMesh.verges(row)` adds it to the verge of that side, so the ribbon's own outer column stands at the place's edge: no new mesh and no new triangle. The edge works (the fill, a retaining wall) start at that edge as at any verge, and the importer has carved the land to the same strip. `WorldGround.vergeAt(index, sign, t)` is the one width the physics reads (the road's height out to it, the fill from it, the wall probe past it), linear between two rows as the ribbon is drawn. So the drawn ground and the physics ground are one surface.
- **What it looks like.** The near road material takes a `roadPad` vertex attribute (the pad on each side and its surface, `RoadSurface.PAD`): the hard standing is the road's own surface a little lighter, or gravel, with a broken white line along the carriageway's edge on asphalt, and the verge's gravel and grass start at the place's outer edge. It is the ribbon itself, so nothing lies over it and nothing z-fights. Beyond 400 m (the flat LOD1 ribbon) the place is the carved land in the worn verge's colour.
- **Its props** (`PlaceMesh`, `js/world/placemesh.js`, one row of `PlaceMesh.KIT` a kind; not part of `RoadMesh.edgeWorks`). A viewpoint: a course of stone 0.35 m high along the pad's outer edge with a two-bar rail on it, and a bench facing out over the drop, its back to the road. A layby: a low kerb along the outer edge, a white marker post with a dark band at each end of the pad, and a waste bin. A car park: the lines of its bays across the pad (parallel bays, one every 5.5 m) and a waste bin. No text, no sign, no name. The props of the places within 600 m of the bike are one mesh in `RoadMesh.material()` (vertex colours, the haze), built again when that set changes: one draw call. Only the open places have props. The props stand at the pad's outer edge, behind the slots the riders wait in.
- **The props are solid** where a bike can reach them: the stone course, the kerb, the bench, the posts and the bin are rects that `Scenery.houseAt` answers with (`PlaceMesh.at`), so the bike brushes or crashes as at a house's wall, and the crash names it ("Rode into a bench"). The bay lines and the rail's bars over the stone are not separate solids.
- **Off the road in a place.** In a place of either source the strip from the carriageway's edge to the verge and `depth` past it is the verge to the bike (`Vehicle.worldSurface` reads `vergeAt(..., safe)`: `safeN`, `safeP` on the points): `benchDrag` and nothing else, so no land drag, no speed cap, no "Too fast on the grass" and no steep-land clock. A wall, a drop and water stay what they are; the importer finds no place on them. The carriageway is unchanged, so traffic's lane (`WorldRoute.laneCurve`), the crew, the roadworks' lanes and the hairpin assist (carriageway only) do not treat a place as road. The wrong-way rule reads speed over 3 m/s against the route and the off-route rule a lateral over `Route.OFF_ROUTE_M`: a rider at rest beside the edge trips neither.
- **Nothing is planted in a place.** `RoadClearance` adds each place's strip as a band of its own along the strip's middle, so trees, boulders, tufts and marker stones keep their margins from it as from a road.
- **A place in the works is closed.** `WorldPlaces.close` takes the roadworks sites and the stage's two ends: a place within 20 m of one is not in `list` (its ground stays ground). A stage holds only the places inside it.
- **Limits by rule.** No place stands on a structure, in a bend under 40 m radius (60 m for a found one), or at a side road's mouth on its side, so no place meets a junction's paving, a hairpin's held inner edge or a deck.

The triangle budget: within 400 m a place's pad costs nothing (the ribbon's columns move). The props cost about 180 triangles at a layby, 432 at a viewpoint and 144 at the car park: 4 044 for the 19 open places of the Tremola slice, of which only the places within 600 m of the bike are drawn (`PlaceMesh.triangles`, which `counts()` and so `world-budget` read): 576 to 1 944 triangles at the three places of `world-place-shots`, in one mesh. `world-budget` reads 700 to 2 350 more triangles than before on the first 5 km (the props near the bike and the far ribbon's kept rows of the new pads) and about 1 000 more past it; it fails at s 2 000 and 4 000 (459 660 and 452 679 against 450 000) as it did before (457 633 and 450 326). Beyond 400 m the flat ribbon keeps every row of a place and the row either side (`rowsOf` marks them `keep`): there the works lay the ground under the verge, and across a wide verge a row every 16 m made flat triangles of 44 m2 (the stacked hairpins' rule is 20 m2). That costs the Tremola's car park 170 triangles (820 against 650 for its two sections) while it is far. The Tremola slice's one map place is in a window of about 325 000 triangles (`world-budget` at km 12), far from the tight one at km 4 (448 103, as before).

Proofs: `tests/unit/world-places.test.js` (the rules on a straight synthetic road; on the slice every place visited: each slot off the carriageway on the drawn ground, a bike at rest and braking along it never on the land, nothing planted in the strip, a car park's, a layby's and a viewpoint's ribbon at its edge with the physics ground on it (within 0.05 m: a layby cut into a bank has the 8 m land triangles up to 0.04 m over its back) and no land over it, the same triangle count with and without the place; every open place has its kind's props, solid, off the carriageway and out of the slots), `tests/unit/world-places-import.test.js` (the importer's rules, the bencher, the synthetic world's car park, `verify.mjs places` on the committed data and on planted faults), the GPU probe `world-place-shots` (a layby, a viewpoint and the car park, or `--ids all`, from the chase view 8 m short of the place with the riders waiting in it, and from across the road: `~/Library/Caches/twisty-world/places-shots`), and the GPU probes `world-budget`, `world-junctions`, `world-floating` and `world-watertight`, which read the place as part of the road (the last two fail at 8389f90 on counts that the places do not change: 2 495 open edges and 1 050 physics points; 117 wall tops and 236 feet).

## Sides and traffic

One convention, defined in `Route` (`js/world/route.js`): lateral runs along `(cos h, -sin h)`, which the chase camera shows on the left of the screen, so `Route.LEFT` is +1, `Route.RIGHT` is -1 and a heading that grows turns left (`Route.turnSide`). Junction prompts, the hairpin advice chevron, the pass signposts (the rider's right first) and traffic read their side through it. The legs' terrain sides and lanes and the road styles' edge keys (`RoadSurface.styles`) still call +lateral "right"; they are labels only. The world gave its oncoming car the lane named "left" meaning the rider's left, which the leg vocabulary put at -lateral, the rider's right: every world car drove on the left, and the prompts and the chevron named each turn the wrong way.

### The crew's pace

Each character has `traits` in `CHARACTERS` (js/vehicle.js), 1 to 5: the pace on `country` roads, on `mountain` roads and round `hairpins`, and how `daring` over a jump. `place` is the rider's home place in the group, 1 at the front; the group ride reads it (`group.md`). Of the player's physics the traits touch one number: `daring` sets the lean limit (`Vehicle.leanOf`; `.agent/system/handling.md`, "The lean limit by the rider"). The multipliers of `Vehicle.physicsOf` do not read them (`tests/unit/characters.test.js` pins those of every rider on every bike).

A trait gives a pace by one table, `Traffic.PACES` (`CREW_PACES` in js/traffic.js). A trait between two rows takes a pace between them (`Traffic.paceOf`).

| Trait | Name | Base speed (m/s) | Spread (m/s) | Corner (m/s^2) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | slow | 30 | 5 | 2.4 |
| 2 | steady | 33 | 5 | 2.8 |
| 3 | average | 36 | 5 | 3.2 |
| 4 | fast | 42 | 6 | 3.6 |
| 5 | expert | 48 | 8 | 4.0 |

Rows 5, 4, 3 and 1 are the old paces by seat (expert, fast, average, slow); row 2 is new. `corner` is the most sideways acceleration the rider takes a world's bend at; the whole crew cornered at 4 before. The speed is the base speed plus the rider's share of the spread (one draw at the start, kept for the ride), times the place's `trafficSpeedScale` (0.4 in a world).

- **On a leg** a rider takes the mean of its `country` and `mountain` traits, once, at the start.
- **In a world** a rider takes its trait for the kind of road it is on, each frame (`AIMotorcycle.takePace`, `Traffic.roadTrait`). `WorldRoute.kindAt(s)` gives the kind as three shares that sum to 1, measured from the samples (`WorldRoute.KIND`):
  - `hairpin`: the road turns 150 degrees or more inside 80 m around s (the rule of `findHairpins` in tools/world/verify.mjs). From 110 degrees it counts in part.
  - `mountain`: over the next 200 m the road climbs or falls at 6 % or more, or its bends turn 0.012 rad a metre or more in sum, or half of it or more is cobbled. Each counts in part from 3 %, 0.004 rad a metre and the first cobbles. The most of the three is taken.
  - `country`: the rest.
  - Both measures are averaged over 60 m of road, so the pace changes over a distance. A hairpin first takes the most of its measure within 30 m, so the whole hairpin is one and the 60 m before and after it are the blend.
- The Tremola slice is 62 % mountain, 30 % hairpin and 8 % country (its two ends).
- In a world the crew rides as a group (`group.md`): it grids in the order of its places, keeps its gaps, passes toward its places, and stops and waits by the group's rules, on top of the following rule of "Contact". On a leg the start order, the passes and the pack rules are as before.
- The pace draws nothing from `Math.random` after the start, and the start draws as before: one draw a rider for the pace, one for the start segment.

A new character with `traits` and a new world need no code: a character without traits rides the middle of the scale (3), and `kindAt` reads only the route's samples. `tests/unit/crew-pace.test.js` holds the table, the kinds on made roads and on the Tremola slice, the order of the riders on each kind of road, and that neither the speed nor the heading has a step.

Traffic keeps right: a vehicle going toward increasing s keeps to `Route.RIGHT`, one going against s to `Route.LEFT` (`Route.keepRight`). A world vehicle drives `WorldRoute.laneCurve`: the uniform cubic B-spline through each row's drawn lane point (`P + l n`, the ribbon's own frame), so its heading and yaw rate have no step where the 4 m polyline turns (0.66 rad a sample in the 6 m hairpins) and it stays inside each corner of the drawn lane. It moves by the line's own length, and looks ahead over its braking distance for the speed each bend allows (`LANE_DRIVE` in `js/traffic.js`: a car corners at 2.5 m/s^2, a crew rider at 4 at most, by its traits ("The crew's pace"), so about 3 m/s on the inside lane of a 6 m hairpin). A crew rider passing swings 2 m toward the centre line and back over 40 m of road by sin^4 and keeps its lane otherwise. `tests/unit/world-traffic.test.js` holds this: every placing on its own half, and every type through every hairpin with no yaw-rate step, its body along its path and every wheel on the drawn carriageway. Leg traffic is unchanged (bound by `tests/unit/legroute-equivalence.test.js`).

A world rider never races the player (`AIMotorcycle.update`): it starts no pass because the player is near, and it does not speed up while the player is alongside or coming past. It closes up only on a player who is `CHASE_FROM_M` to `CHASE_TO_M` ahead of it. The legs' old rule (a third faster and a pass within 10 m of the player, in any direction) swung the rider toward the centre line into a player who overtook there (`tests/unit/world-traffic.test.js`). A rule for anything that moves a rider across the road: never toward a vehicle that is beside it or closing from behind.

### Contact

In a world the bikes and the cars are solid to each other (`CONTACT` in js/traffic.js, `Traffic.CONTACT`: every size and threshold, in one block). A leg is as it was: its crew are not solid (they chase and pass the player by the legs' own rules, which would run them into its back), and its car is the 2.2 m ball about its origin.

- **The shape is the vehicle**: a box in the ground plane about the model's origin, tested box against box (`Traffic.overlap`, the four box axes; `Traffic.reachAlong` is the depth along one line). One test for the player against a rider, the player against a car, and the crew and cars against each other (`Traffic.overlaps`). Two boxes meet only within `heightM` of each other. The ball it replaces hit the player 0.85 m clear of a car's side and missed a bike at its wheels.

| Kind | Length (m) | Width (m) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bike | 2.1 | 0.8 | 1.4 m of wheelbase and a 0.3 m tyre at each end; the bar |
| car | 4.2 | 1.9 | the body |

- **Following** (`Traffic.holdForRoadworks`, every frame, with or without roadworks). `stopM` is the room to whatever is ahead going the same way (and to a red light), less both halves and `QUEUE_GAP_M`: a vehicle never moves past it (`laneMetres`). `planM` is that room plus the distance the vehicle ahead needs to stop at its own hardest braking, and `laneSpeed` plans the speed by it. So a vehicle closes up on one that moves, brakes as it brakes, and stops behind it when it stops: between two riders at v m/s the origins are 4 + v^2/12 m apart along the ride (4.8 m at 3 m/s, 16 m at 12, 31 m at 18). Before, the room was planned as if the vehicle ahead stood (4 + v^2/6). A rider parked out of sight at the end of the ride is behind nobody.
- **The player is ahead of a vehicle** going its way when its origin is `follow.aheadM` or more up the road and within `follow.bandM` of that vehicle's lane line across the road (`Traffic.playerLead`, `leadOff`): the crew brake for the player as for a rider, taking the player to brake at `follow.playerBrake`. The player holds no `stopM`: a player who cuts in is braked for as hard as the rider can, never by a dead stop. A player passing a metre to the side is not braked for. A crashed player is a standing thing ahead. Near a roadworks site a player slower than `follow.queueMs` is one of the queue as before (`Roadworks.riderEntry`); a moving one is followed by this rule (as one of the queue it stopped a rider it passed dead).
- **A touch** (`Traffic.contact`, `Traffic.touch`, after everything has moved). The line of the contact is the one of the rider's two axes along which the boxes are less deep: across the road side by side, along it end to end. The closing speed is the player's speed across the rider's heading, or its speed over the rider's.
  - **Bump** (closing `hardMs` or less): the player is set clear of the rider along that line, keeps `bump.keep` of its speed (end to end, at most the rider's speed), and rolls `bump.wobble` rad away (`Vehicle.contactWobble`). The rider moves `bump.shoveM` off its lane line, away from the player (`AIMotorcycle.nudge`, at most `nudge.mostM`, its box on its own half and on the carriageway, nothing in a roadworks single lane), and comes back only once the player is `nudge.holdM` away. One contact is one bump: for `bump.cooldownS` the boxes are only held apart. The game says "Bumped <name>".
  - **Crash** (closing over `hardMs`): `Vehicle.worldCrash('Rode into <name>', 'lowside', 'rider')`; the bike slides on at the rider's speed less `crash.throw` of the closing speed. The rider keeps `crash.slowTo` of its pace for `crash.slowS`, shakes, and rides on. It does not go down, so it never lies in the road, and it cannot be hit for `crash.ghostS`.
  - **The rider ran into the player's back** (the following rule failed it): the rider takes the player's speed and drops back clear. The player loses nothing. Never a crash: the player could not see it.
- **Where the player is put down** (`Traffic.clearAround`, `clearRiders`: a restart in place, a debug skip): a rider from `restart.backM` behind to `restart.aheadM` ahead moves to the first free place from `restart.slotM` ahead, by a fixed rule; one up to `restart.settleM` behind takes the speed its place allows; one within `restart.ghostM` cannot be hit for `crash.ghostS`. Riders moved out of one roadworks lane stand behind each other before its entry, and `restart.backM` or more from the player when the player stands there (`clearOfRoadworks`: a station is a site's entry, so a put-down at one is at that place).
- **Rules for anything new.** A new character, bike or world has contact with no code; a bike model that is longer or wider carries `contact: {length, width}` in `BIKE_MODELS`. A crash needs something the player rode into: no rule may crash the player for what came from behind or from the side into it. Nothing that moves a rider across the road may move it toward a bike beside it. No draw from `Math.random`: a world's traffic draws nothing after it is built (the legs' pack spread drew each frame in a world too, and was never read there).
- `tests/unit/contact.test.js` holds the box test; `tests/unit/world-contact.test.js` rides the real slice (measured apart from the game's own test, `tests/helpers/world-contact.js`): a pass at 1 m, a bump, a crash that names the rider, the rider riding on, the crew queued behind a slow player for a kilometre, the whole crew, the car and the player over 3.5 km with hairpins and red lights with no two boxes in each other in any frame, the start, a restart onto the crew, and the draws.

## Every road near the bike looks like a road

Every road within 400 m of the bike (`WorldStreamer.ROAD_LOD_M[0]`) is drawn at LOD0, whatever its role: the ride, the stubs, the side roads, and the road before the start and past the finish. LOD0 is the textured asphalt or setts, the markings of the road's swissTLM3D class (`RoadSurface.styles`, the same rules as the ride) and a verge on each side. Beyond 400 m the flat LOD1 ribbon is enough.

- A side chunk's roads are cut into pieces of 32 samples (about 128 m, `WorldEnvironment.sidePieces`). A piece is LOD0 when its centre line's box comes within `SIDE_NEAR_M + SIDE_PAD_M` (410 m) of the bike, and it stays LOD0 until the box is `SIDE_SLACK_M` (200 m) farther (`WorldStreamer.sideNear`). When that set changes, only the chunk's ribbons are built again (`WorldEnvironment.drawSideRibbons`; `sideRuns` cuts each road into LOD0 and LOD1 runs). A side road's structures and edge works stand at its verge's outer edge at every level of detail, as the ride's do, so they are built once per chunk. A chunk is still one mesh for each material.
- A side road at LOD0 keeps LOD1's rows (every 16 m, sooner in a bend) and has five columns, not seven: no physics rides it, and the window's triangle budget is tight. Its structures and edge works stand on the same rows (`RoadMesh.vergeSides`), and each road keeps the ribbon of every run it has drawn, so a change of level of detail merges kept geometry.
- The ride's data ends at the stage's ends, but the graph keeps the road beyond. Where the road goes on through the ride's end node (no junction there), that side edge starts on the ride's end row, is as wide as the ride there and narrows to its own width over `RIDE_TAPER_M` (30 m), and its metres along go on from the ride's (`WorldEnvironment.fromRideEnd`). So the finish line is not the end of the world.
- `tests/unit/world-road-look.test.js` checks every road ribbon triangle within the radius of the start, the finish and each station. `node tools/terrain-check.mjs world-road-look --world tremola --gpu` counts flat road pixels within the radius in the views at the finish, the start and on a side road.

## The land meets every retaining wall

The rule: the land in front of a wall meets its foot, level; the road behind meets its top; no land stands in front of a wall's face; a wall is one run from end to end and ends in the ground.

The land is shaped to the wall, not the wall to the land. The wall stands where the road's edge is: the physics stops the bike there and the parapet is on it, so it cannot move. The land is an 8 m height grid, which cannot hold a wall: the triangle from the last node in front of a wall to the first node behind it was a ramp of land through the stone.

- **Where a wall stands** (`WorldEnvironment.edgeFoot` `wall`, read from the tile's own nodes only): over a lower road; where the importer's drop zone lies past the verge and the full-detail land lies under the edge; where the land lies `WALL_OVER_M` (1.5 m) or more under a slope at the batter anywhere in its 6 m run. ## Land and the tunnels

The owner: "handle terrain colliding with tunnels better - both at the entrances and inside the tunnels". The importer's land is the terrain model with the roads carved into it. Over a tunnel it is the mountain, and over a gallery it is the gallery's own roof fill, 4 to 7 m over the road: the tube RoadMesh draws round the road is 6.5 m high (`WorldRoute.ROOF_M`), so the land stood inside the galleries along their whole length, and through the valley wall of a gallery on a hillside. One rule for every tube of every world, the ride's and the side roads':

- **The land is cut to the tube's box** (`TerrainMesh.tubeClip`, at every level of detail). The box of each segment between two rows of a tunnel or gallery run is its walls' feet in plan (the ride's `RoadMesh.TUBE_WALL_M`, 1.6 m, past half its width; a side road's verge edges) from `TUBE_UNDER_M` (5 cm) under the floor to `TUBE_OVER_M` (3 cm) over the roof (`TerrainMesh.tubeBox`). What land lies in it is left out, and a triangle the box cuts is cut along its faces: the land outside the walls, under the floor and over the roof stays. `WorldEnvironment.tubesNear` gives a tile its boxes: the ride's from the route, the side roads' from the chunks drawn now (`setSideTubes`: the tiles under them are drawn again when they change, and a tube no longer drawn cuts nothing).
- **What the land no longer closes, the tube does** (`RoadMesh.tubeWorks`, `headwall`, `sidePortal`):
  - the ride's floor: a concrete strip from `TUBE_FLOOR_M` (0.3 m) past the carriageway's edge out to each wall, over the verge, which thins out there;
  - a footing: each wall's face down to `TUBE_FOOT_M` under the land at its foot, where the land lies under the floor (a gallery on a hillside stands on its wall, not over a gap);
  - a headwall over each portal's beam, in the portal's plane, up to the land just behind it (at most `TUBE_HEAD_M`, 14 m over the road): the step from the beam to the land over the roof is fronted, not seen through;
  - a side road's tube has a portal frame (beam and posts) where its run ends, and none where the road goes on as a tube past the piece drawn.
- **Nothing else stands in a tube.** No ground cover in a box (`TerrainMesh.inTube`). The importer already carves the approach to each portal to the road (no land over the road in front of a mouth).
- **Proofs.** `tests/unit/world-tunnels.test.js` (the rule on a slope of land, and every tube of the Tremola and the Gotthard leg as drawn, the bike put down every 200 m and every tube within 500 m read: `tests/helpers/world-tunnels.js`, `TUNNEL_LIMITS` all 0): `inside` land in a box, `floor` a gap in the ride's floor out to the walls, `mouth` land over the road within 10 m in front of a portal, `face` land over the roof behind a portal with nothing in its plane to front it. The GPU probe `node tools/terrain-check.mjs world-tunnels --world <stage> --gpu [--out <dir>]` reads the same in the page and saves three pictures of every portal (from the road 28 m out, from the side and above, and from 14 m inside).

## Arched viaducts

The owner asked for an arched viaduct "where walls hold the road on both sides", with stone arches in 10 m bays, semicircular where there is room, and arches in the stack of hairpins at km 7.9 to 8.1 with their piers on the hillside between the legs. The rule is one table, `RoadMesh.VIADUCT`, read on any world; no place is named in code. `WorldEnvironment.surveyViaducts` picks and lays out each viaduct, `RoadMesh.viaduct` builds it, and `TerrainMesh.pitCuts` lays the ground under it.

- **The stretch.** Both edges of the ride stand on a wall at the same rows for `minM` (24 m) or more. A row's edge is walled where the land asks for a wall `dropM` (8 m) deep or more (`RoadMesh.wallEnd`, the wall plan's own test, read on the rows as the data gives them, `rawBits`), and the plan's own joins hold: a segment is walled where either of its rows is, and two walls of an edge under `gapM` (`WALL_GAP_M`, 12 m) apart are one. Nothing else stands in it or within `clearM` (12 m) of it: a structure, a side road's mouth or a stopping place. No roadworks site lies within `worksM` (30 m). The arches stand on the longest part of it that bends no sharper than `curveMax` (1/40 rad a metre), one row in from each end, so that the walls of both edges go on into the viaduct's two ends.
- **Once, whole.** A stretch is judged when the tiles and the side roads' chunks round all of it are here, and never again (`viaductDone`, `viaductEdge`). So the order the window builds in does not change it. Its rows then carry the bridge bit and the layout (`point.viaduct`), and every plan, cut and section that read them is made again.
- **The ground.** Each row's ground is the lower of the land `groundM` (0.5, 2 and 4 m) out from each face, the tiles' own nodes, and never lower than another road beside or under it (its surface less `SINK_M`). It is the lowest within half a bay, so one arch stands on one ground and not on a step at every row.
- **The bays.** The viaduct is cut into whole bays of `spanM` (10 m), from `spanMin` to `spanMax` (8 to 12 m). A pier is `pierShare` (1/6) of its bay thick, `pierMinM` (1.2 m) at the least. An arch opens between the half piers at its bay's ends. It has `crownM` (0.9 m) of stone over its crown under the road, and it springs `springM` (1.5 m) or more over the ground under it. It is semicircular where that leaves room and a face can open; else it springs as high as a face opens, segmental, down to a rise of `riseMin` (1/6) of its span. Its springing stands `headroomM` (4.5 m) over any road under it. Its piers stand in no road and no road's verge (`PIER_CLEAR_M` past it). A bay that fails one of these ends the viaduct at an abutment, and the part left is laid out again; a viaduct of fewer than `minBays` (2) is none. The layout keeps what each bay lacked (`why`: `room`, `headroom`, `pier`, `opening`).
- **A face opens or is blind.** A face opens under an arch where the ground in front of it lies `openM` (0.3 m) or more under the springing all along the opening. Elsewhere it goes down to the ground, a blind arch: in a stack of hairpins the uphill face stands against the hillside and the leg above, so its arches open on the downhill side only and the vault's back is the blind face's stone. The blind face is plain stone from the parapet down into the land. Seen from the valley, the upper viaduct's open arches (km 8.08) stand behind the lower leg's parapet, a couple of metres in front of them: from low down only their crowns show over it. That is the open side, not the blind one.
- **The stone** (`RoadMesh.viaduct`, in the road works' mesh as group 4, and the stone under its deck as group 5: both the retaining walls' own material, `RoadMesh.stoneMaterial`). It is laid and lit as a retaining wall's: each face carries `wallFace` 1 with the ride's metres along it and each column's outward as its normal (`face`, as `RoadMesh.edgeWorks` lays a wall's face), and its parapet's outer face takes the same metres (`rail`'s `along`), so its courses, block size, colour and light are a wall's and a viaduct reads as the masonry of the walls that run into it. Laid by the world's own horizontal (`wallFace` 0) with each quad's own normal, its faces read as another stone beside the walls (the owner's picture at km 7.9). Each face stands where the retaining walls' faces stood (`VIADUCT_FACE_M`: the verge's edge and `STONE_PROUD_M`), so a road beside it meets it as it met the wall. Over each arch the face comes down to the arch; between the arches and where it is blind it goes down into the land, `sinkM` (0.5 m) deep. Each arch's underside runs from face to face, each pier's two sides stand across under it from the land to the stone over the crowns, and that stone is closed from face to face `crownM` under the road. The parapet is a bridge's (`parapetW` 0.3 m wide, `parapetH` 1.1 m high) on the faces' tops. ## A side road is open

The owner: "I don't like the angled barriers- just remove them?" Nothing closes a side road: no barrier is drawn and none is solid. Every side road that leaves a ride junction (`WorldGraph.sideStubs`) is drawn from the junction as its stub, the first `WorldGraph.STUB_M` (60 m) at LOD0, and the rest of its edge on from the stub's end; the physics rides it as drawn (`WorldGround.setSideEdges`). The importer still writes `passes[].barriers` into `graph/junctions.json`; the game no longer reads them.

- **What brings a rider back** is the off-route rule (`WorldGuide`, `Route.offRoute`). More than `Route.OFF_ROUTE_M` (40 m) from the ride, or riding against it over `REVERSE_SPEED` for `REVERSE_WARN_S`, says "Wrong way" once in the call-out stack; `RESTART_AFTER_S` (10 s) after that first warning the rider is put back where the bike left the road, 5 s down: "Off route, back on the road +5 s" (or "Wrong way, turned round +5 s"). The clock runs from the first warning: a rider who went from the wrong way to off the line was given 10 s more. The distance is the distance from the ride (`projection.distance`), not the lateral: past the finish the lateral is along the last row's normal, and a rider at km 13.1 stayed "on route" for 290 m. - **How far a rider gets.** At 10 m/s from 40 m before the junction, along each of the 26 side roads of the Tremola slice and on through the roads they meet: the rule puts the rider back 113 to 230 m from the junction (the median 146 m), 10 s after the warning. No rider leaves the land the world has in detail (`WorldGround.mapped`) first.
- **Nothing on a side road stops a rider that the road does not draw.** Before, a rider on four side roads met a wall or a drop within 30 m of the junction: the bike's zone was rebuilt from the ride's row and the lateral (`WorldGround.position`), 2 to 7 m from the bike on a bend 10 to 30 m out, off the side road or over a drop. - **What happens.** Black bars slide in at the top and the foot of the screen (CSS bars over the canvas, as near 2.39:1 as a 16:9 screen gives and at most `BAR_MOST` (12%) of the height each, so a tall phone keeps most of its picture; `StopScene.barPx`), and the HUD and the map fade out. The result card comes up in the lower bar `CARD_AFTER_S` after the player parks, with Next leg (N, when a leg opens after this one), Photo (V), Restart (R) and Menu (M). Any key or a tap before the card skips to it with everyone parked and off.
- **The camera.** Behind the player as it rides in (`CHASE`), then the path of the stop's kind (`KINDS`): keys in the stop's own frame (the origin at the middle of the slots, `out` from the way in to there, or a lookout's `view`, `side` square to it); a key with `edge` measures `out` from the area's outline (the rail). - **What riders do.** A character's `stop` list (CHARACTERS in js/vehicle.js): `vape` for the three the owner named, a small pale cloud from the helmet's front every `PUFF_EVERY_S`, no brand and nothing else shown. Anyone can take a picture: the card's Photo opens photo mode (js/world/photo.js) round the player's bike.
- **A stop behind the line** (`keepLine`, the Tremola's hospice square, 644 m before its line): the player rides on `TURN_M` (12 m) past where it is, turns across the road and rides back on the other half to the turn-in. A long way in is ridden in `WAY_MOST_S` (24 s) at the most, braking at `BRAKE_MS2` (3 m/s^2); the card comes up once every rider is off.

## Free roam

A world is ridden in one of two game modes (`GameModes`, js/gamemodes.js): **Time trial** (the timed ride, as before) and **Free roam**. The rider screen has a row for them over the rider mode. Free roam is locked ("coming later") and open in debug mode only (`Game.debugEnabled`); a locked choice is a time trial. The address can ask for it with `&mode=roam`, read in js/ridemode.js alone, and the fresh page of a world chosen after a leg keeps the mode and `debug`. Free roam is a mode of the world, not of a stage: it reads the world's graph and tiles, so every importer world has it.

What free roam changes (`Game.startFreeRoam`, `updateFreeRoam`, `restartRoam`; `FreeRoam`, js/world/freeroam.js):
- **No clock and no ride.** No stations, splits, finish or stage progress (`updateWorldRun` is not called; the HUD's clock and progress are hidden by `body.free-roam`). No cut rule and no off-route or wrong-way rule (`WorldGuide.roam`): a U-turn is allowed anywhere. A free-roam ride never finishes, so it never submits a time.
- **No barrier on a side road.** None stands since the side roads were opened; `world-roam` fails on one in the scene.
- **The camera follows the rider.** (A fresh page starts in the Onboard view, the rider's own eye, where the bike is the bars at the foot of the frame; in the Standard chase on a side road at 11 m/s `world-roam` measures the bike at (0.04, -0.31) of the frame, -1 to 1 each way, and fails past 0.6.) `WorldCamera.followRider`: the line the camera reads (`centre`, `curvature`) is the rider's own, straight through the bike along its way (its yaw, eased at `followRate`), not the ride. The boom, the look, the land and the walls rules are the same.
- **The land follows the bike.** The streamer already holds the tiles within 900 m of the bike, the ride's sections and the side roads within 1.2 km of it by place; the ride window is read at the nearest place on the ride (`Game.worldStreamS`).
- **Any drawn road.** The physics has a side road's ground (`WorldGround.sideRoadY`), its zone (a road: no `runoff`) and its crashes. The bike's height on a side road past the ride's carriageway is that road's own surface (`Vehicle.surfaceAt`): before free roam it was the ride's height beside it, the same near a junction (where the off-route rule kept a rider) and 17 m over the road 200 m down one. Far off the ride the bike's place on the ride is found again over the whole ride each frame (`FreeRoam.FAR_M`). A side road lacks what is the ride's own: the hairpin assist and its advice, the road surface flags (the setts, wet patches, the edge line) and `route.project` (the projection stays on the ride).
- **A restart** puts the bike back where it last was on a road (the ride or a side road: no `runoff`), a second before the crash, facing the way it was going (`FreeRoam.restartPose`). No penalty: there is no clock.
- **The roadworks are off.** Their lights work one lane in the ride's direction; in free roam the sites are hidden and their rules dropped.
- **The edge of the map.** The detailed land is the data's height tiles; past them the far land has no physics. Within `SOFT_M` (150 m) of the land's outline the bike is slowed as on deep grass and told "Edge of the map: turn back"; within `HARD_M` (40 m), or outside it, it is put back on the last road it was on, turned round ("Turned back at the edge of the map"). A road of the data that runs to the land's edge (on the shipped slice 25 of the 360 corners of the road chunks' boxes lie within the soft band or past it) meets the edge rule before it ends, so no barrier model is placed at a road's end; a road that ends inside the land is a real dead end, and the land past it is ridden as land. The edge is never a fall and never NaN (`tests/unit/free-roam.test.js`).
- **The mini map** is the one of the time trial: north up, the bike in the middle, the ride and the side roads in residence.

- **Photo mode** (`js/world/photo.js`, `PhotoMode`, `Game.openPhoto`, `Game.savePhoto`): V while riding, or Photo (V) on the pause panel. The ride pauses, the HUD and the touch controls go, and a camera of the rider's own goes round the bike: A and D (or the arrows) round, W and S up and down (-0.15 to 1.35 rad), + and - closer and further (2.5 to 30 m); it never goes into the ground (0.3 m over it) and looks at the rider's middle. C saves the canvas as a PNG named by the time alone (`twisty-20261125-090507.png`, no place). Escape or V go back to where it opened from. The bar is a dialog of the stack: `modal-check` rides `roam-pause-photo` and `roam-photo-key`; `world-roam` takes the camera round to the bike's front left and saves a picture (`roam-photo.jpg`). A paused frame is drawn with the photo camera (`Game.animate`).
- **Traffic on the side roads** (`js/world/sidetraffic.js`, `SideTraffic`): a car on each side road whose junction is within 700 m of the bike (4 at most; one past 900 m goes), once the road's rows have streamed in. It drives 400 m out from the junction (or to the road's end) and back, at 9 m/s, 1.3 m right of the road's middle, on the drawn ground, and stands 3 s at each end. With the bike ahead of it on its way (within 22 m ahead and 4 m across) it brakes to a stand, so it never drives into the bike. The bike that rides into a car crashes faster than 3 m/s ("Rode into a car") and stops against it slower. The cars are the ride's car model (`Car.createCarModel`) in plain colours (`Game.SIDE_CAR_COLORS`). `world-roam` fails with no car out while it rides a side road, a car more than 0.3 m off the drawn ground, or a crash on a car; its `roam-sidecar.jpg` looks down at the nearest from behind it.

All eight options the owner asked for are built.

Proofs: `tests/unit/free-roam.test.js` (the mode and its lock, the edge on a synthetic block of tiles, the restart, the follow camera's line, the guide's rules off, the streamer holding tiles round a bike 2.5 km off the ride), `tests/unit/ride-mode.test.js` (`mode=roam`), and `node tools/terrain-check.mjs world-roam --world tremola --gpu --query 'mode=roam&debug'` (free roam on, no barrier, a ride off the ride and down the side road that runs furthest from it, 184 m off the ride on its own surface, turned back at the edge, pictures of the start, the side road and the menu's row).

## One haze for everything far away

Distant land read green: the old tint went toward a grey-blue and reached 63% only at 14 km, so land 3 to 8 km off kept its green (hue 170 to 180) in front of a sky that was bluer than it. The photos say otherwise (`node tools/world/photomatch.mjs haze`, 19 photos; mean display colour): land at 1 to 3 km is still its own colour (90, 95, 88), at 3 to 8 km it is 76, 98, 121 (hue 211), from 8 km 92, 125, 170 (hue 214), and the sky just over the skyline is 188, 203, 224 (hue 215, saturation 0.16). Distant land takes the sky's hue, deeper and darker, and is never greener than the sky.

- **One term.** `LandShade.HAZE` and `worldHaze` (GLSL) are the one aerial perspective of a world: the fragment loses its own saturation and goes toward the air's colour by `most * (1 - exp(-tau^onset))`, before the scene fog. `tau` is the optical depth of the ray (`perM` per metre at `baseY`, thinning with height over `scaleM`). `onset` 2 keeps the first kilometres clear, as the photos are.
- **The air's colour** takes its hue from the scene fog (the sky at the horizon) at the saturation and value of `HAZE.air`; in mist, rain or snow it is the weather's own colour (`LandShade.airColor`).
- **Everything a world draws has it.** Land, far land, roads, walls and trees get it from `LandShade.patch`. Any other material (a house, a boulder, a sign) gets it from `LandShade.haze`: `LandShade.update` gives it to every material under the world's group each frame, before it is first drawn. A model's materials are shared with the legs, so a world draws a copy (`LandShade.adopt`). A new kind of world object needs no code for the haze.
- **The sky.** A world's sky keeps the horizon's pale colour higher than a leg's (`WorldLooks.horizonSpan`, `Graphics.setHorizonSpan`): in the photos the sky over ridges 5 to 15 degrees up is still pale; with a leg's span it was the mid sky's blue.
- **Checks.** `tests/unit/world-haze.test.js` holds the term's shape and that materials take it. The GPU probe `world-haze` draws six views in clear air and reads the land by its distance: each band's mean colour must be in the photos' range, no band from 3 km may be greener than the sky near the horizon (green less blue, 0.02 of tolerance), and nothing drawn may lack the haze. A new world must pass it with its own views (`--views s:turn,...`).

## The chase camera swings with the lean, goes under bridges and stops short of walls

- **Swing.** A world's boom turns by the route, not by the bike's yaw. It still takes the legs' carving swing in the boom's frame: the camera moves sideways with the lean and its look point leads into the corner, both lagged (`Game.worldCameraInput` `swing`, `WorldCamera.update`). The land and the road ahead scale the sideways shift down (`swingShare`), so the rock and ground limits stay on top. `tests/unit/world-camera-swing.test.js` holds it to the legs' swing on the same weave.
- **Lower at speed.** A world's boom keeps its mode's height up to `speedLowerFromMs` (22 m/s, about 50 mph) and comes down by `speedLowerShare` (50%) of it by `speedLowerMs` (45 m/s, about 100 mph), eased (`WorldCamera.speedHeight`): the standard view from 3 m to 1.5 m, the high view from 8 m to 4 m. It is a high-speed thing: at the pace of the bends the view is the mode's own. A leg's camera keeps its slight rise.
- **Under a road overhead.** `WorldEnvironment.overheadY(x, z, y)` gives the underside of the lowest road over a place (a side road's bridge, a viaduct, another leg on its deck), from the same index of carriageways the structures use, so a new world needs no list of bridges. The camera reads the road `roofAheadS` of riding ahead (2.6 s, 30 m at the least) and brings the boom's height down over that whole approach to pass `roofClearM` under the deck, as high as it can be there (and `roofFastM` lower again at speed, between `roofFastFrom` and `roofFastTo`), then back up slowly (`roofIn`, `roofOut`): a change of height over the approach, never a drop at the bridge (the first version came down in half a second and read as a switch to a low camera). The height allows for the road's grade between the deck and the bike. A hard limit keeps the camera `roofHardM` under the deck. Before, the high view kept its 8 m and flew over the deck with the bike out of sight. `tests/unit/world-camera-bridge.test.js` holds it (never over a deck, no jump, back at its height after). A tunnel's roof is `Game.tunnelRoofY`, as before.

- **Short of a wall.** The camera never goes through a wall or sits behind one. Before, in the high view the boom swung over the retaining wall of the leg above a stacked hairpin; the rule above then put the camera down under that leg in one frame, inside the hill behind the wall's face (a jump of 2 m, and the wall's back on the screen). The land rule does not see a wall: it keeps the boom over the land by height only, and a wall is a face with no width.
  - **What a wall is.** `WorldEnvironment.cameraFaces(x, z, reach)` lists the faces near the bike: the retaining walls of the ride's drawn sections and of the side roads with their parapets where they are drawn (`RoadMesh.wallRuns`, the wall plan the physics reads through `walledAt`; `runParapet`), the face a wall goes on as past its end (`taperAt`, as deep as it holds the fill there, with its wall's parapet: `parapetAt`), the parapets of the ride's bridges, a viaduct's faces and its arches' sides ("Arched viaducts"), and the four walls of every house drawn (`Scenery.houseAt`'s rects). A face is a line in plan with a top and a foot at each end, turned so that it faces out on the right, and a mark when a road stands on it. Nothing is cast at a mesh. The list is made again when the bike has moved `stepM` or one of its sources has changed (`faceStamp`, `Scenery.stamp`); a frame allocates nothing. A new world needs no list of walls.
  - **The rule** (`WorldCamera.wallShare`). A boom that closes on a face's plane, meets it between the face's ends and is not over its top there, ends `clearM` short of the plane. The share of the boom is one more input of the spring that the land already drives (`fractionIn` in, `fractionOut` out: fast in, slow out), with a hard limit `hardM` short of the plane behind it. The swing gives way first: `swingShare` takes the largest step of the swing whose boom the walls and the land shorten no more than the boom with no swing. The same choice also refuses a step whose boom a road overhead brings lower than the boom with no swing: the roof's spring reads the road ahead, not a deck beside it, and with the tuning's swing a 40 m boom's end swung under a side road's deck and the roof's hard limit put the camera down 14 m in one frame. So beside an uphill wall the camera stops swinging toward the wall, and a boom the road's own turn carries into a wall comes in along itself. A boom that runs along a face or leaves it is left alone, so a bike beside a parapet keeps its camera.
  - **Over a low face.** A boom that clears a face's top by `overM` passes over it (the road's own parapet, a low wall): nothing is raised. From `overBandM` more than that the face draws the boom in, eased, and a boom that passes a face's end is drawn in over `endBandM`, so the share moves smoothly as the boom sweeps onto a wall.
  - **Never over a road overhead.** Met from outside, a wall with a road on it is not passed over at any height once that road is `roadTo` over the bike's (eased in from `roadFrom`): from there the road is a road overhead, the camera stays under it, and past the wall it would be in the hill.
  - **A deck is not land.** The ground beside the ride's band is a side road's surface where one lies there, on its bridge over the ride too. `WorldCamera.landY` reads a deck `roadTo` or more over both the ride and the land under it as the land under it. Read as land, a strip of deck cut the boom short under such a bridge in one frame (0.8 m).
  - **Why pull in and not raise.** Pulling in keeps the camera on its own line to the bike, so the view only comes nearer. Raising it over a wall changes the view's pitch, and over the wall of a leg above it puts the camera over that leg, where the roof rule forbids it.
  - **The numbers** are one table, `WorldCamera.WALLS`:

    | Key | Value | What it is |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `clearM` | 0.8 m | The boom ends this far short of a face it closes on (the near plane's corners are 0.46 m from the camera). |
    | `hardM` | 0.5 m | The least distance, held in the same frame. Also how far past a face's end the hard limit holds. |
    | `nearShare` | 0.5 | With the bike nearer a face than the margin, the boom keeps this share of the bike's own distance. |
    | `overM`, `hardOverM` | 0.4 m, 0.2 m | A boom this far over a face's top passes over it (eased, hard). |
    | `overBandM` | 0.6 m | From this much more over the top, the face starts to draw the boom in. |
    | `roadFrom`, `roadTo` | 1.5 m, 2.5 m | The height of a wall's road over the bike's from which the wall is not passed over from outside (`roadTo` is `WorldEnvironment.OVERHEAD_FROM_M`). |
    | `roadUpM` | 60 m | How far over its top such a wall stops a boom. |
    | `endBandM` | 3 m | A boom that passes a face's end is drawn in from this far past it. |
    | `houseM` | 12 m | The height a house is taken to stand over the land under it. |
    | `reachM`, `stepM` | 12 m, 4 m | The faces are listed within two booms and `reachM` of the bike, and again when it has moved `stepM`. |

  - **The tuning.** `camDistance` and `camSwing` multiply the boom and the swing in `Game.worldCameraInput`, before the rule, so a tuned camera is held by the same faces.
  - **Checks.** `tests/unit/world-camera-walls.test.js` rides every hairpin of the shipped stage and of the fixture world with a kinematic bike in both views (5 m/s, 7 m/s, the advised speed, the worst lean each way, 2 m to each side, the tuning at its most) and reads the line from the bike to the camera against the walls, the houses and the land with its own geometry (`tests/helpers/world-camera-walls.js`). The camera's distance may change by 0.5 m a frame at the most at the default boom, and by 5% of itself with the tuning (the land's spring draws a 38 m boom in by 4% at most). The same rides with the rule off find the crossings. The rides run in a node process of their own (`tests/helpers/world-camera-walls-sweep.mjs`): under jest's module VM they take about fifteen times as long, over an hour. The GPU probe `world-camera-walls` rides every hairpin behind the world pilot in both views and casts a ray from the camera to the bike and back at the meshes drawn (`--off` rides without the rule, `--shots s,s,s --out <dir>` takes the worst frame with the rule off and the same tick with it on). The helmet and onboard views have no boom and need none of this.

## No NaN reaches physics or the frame

A bike put down at a ride vertex's exact `s` (a restart, a resume, a probe's put-down: stations sit at sample `s` values) took a NaN height on its first step. The projection lands on the vertex at distance 0, and `Vehicle.updateElevation` blended toward the next vertex, also at distance 0, by 0 / 0. The NaN reached the fog density through `WeatherSystem.fogElevation`, and bloom spread it over the whole frame: 21 of 31 photo-match views drew pure black. A zero total now keeps the projection's height.

Each boundary has one guard, and each guard logs once, loudly. A held value is a bug upstream, not a recovery.
- `Vehicle.update` runs the step (`integrate`) and checks the position, the velocity and `Vehicle.PHYSICS_STATE`. A step that leaves a non-finite number is undone to the last finite state, and `nonFiniteSteps` counts it.
- `WeatherSystem.writeFogDensity`, the only writer of the fog density, keeps the last finite density.
- `AtmosphereTrack.sample` reads the last finite segment for a non-finite one.

`tests/unit/world-finite.test.js` reads every route and ground query at every sample's exact `s`, at `s` +- 1e-9, at both ends and on the ribbon's edges. It puts the bike down at every station and at every sample of every hairpin, and the guard must not fire. The GPU probe `world-vertex` puts the bike down at exact vertices: the fog, the camera and the bike must stay finite, and the frame must be neither black nor one flat colour.

## Determinism

Scenery uses `Environment.rand()` (seeded per leg). The menu preview and the ride must build the same world. Do not use `Math.random()` in `environment.js`.

## Marmots

`js/world/marmots.js` puts small colonies of alpine marmots on the high pastures by the lakes. They come from a world's own data, by rule (`MarmotRule.RULE`), never from a place's name, so a new world gets them where its land and water say so.

- **Where.** A colony needs open ground (grass, rock, scree, or no cover) at 2,000 m or higher. It stands within 140 m of water (a water node or water cover) and no nearer than 4 m. The ground is flat: under about 20 degrees (`steep` 0.06). It is 6 m clear of every road band (`RoadClearance`) and within 35 m of the ride's own band, where a rider passes it. A colony by a side road alone is never seen, so it is not placed. It stands within 4 m of the ride's height beside it, and a rider coming up the ride sees it: the line from 2 m over the ride 30, 50 and 70 m back is clear of the land from two of the three (`Marmots.seen`). There is at most one colony to a 128 m cell, chosen by a hash of the cell. A colony has 2 to 5 animals within 20 m, each 2.5 m from the next. On the shipped stage this gives 5 colonies and 21 animals on the summit plateau, from km 12.3.
- **Life.** An animal sits up and looks out, drops to all fours, scuttles to a second point of its own 2 to 5 m off, and sits again. When the bike comes within 80 m the colony's sentinel (its first animal) sits up on the spot, turned to the bike, and keeps still; the others go on feeding and scuttling (the owner saw a colony of still figures). Every animal has a second point of its own to scuttle to: without one it scuttled on the spot. Within 30 m it runs to its burrow and goes down into it over 0.4 s, and the first of the colony whistles (`Sound.playWhistle`). It comes up again over 0.6 s, 6 s after the bike is 60 m away. The burrow is drawn at each animal's home: a low mound of bare earth round a dark hole that goes down into the ground (`Marmots.burrowGeometry`, 50 triangles, one instanced mesh, the haze on it), so an animal never vanishes in the open. The animals are drawn at 1.3 times their real size, so one reads from the saddle. They are drawn within 260 m of the camera, at most 48 of each pose, as three instanced meshes. Each takes the haze (`LandShade.adopt`). Placed by any road and 15 to 120 m off, the first colonies were never nearer the chase camera than 40 m and were never seen. With this rule the nearest comes within 17 m.
- **Crossing.** A ride draws one crossing at most, in a quarter of rides (`Marmots.CROSSING.chance`). It happens at a colony within 60 m of the road. The road must run straight for 150 m before the place: the turning in all, both ways counted, is under 12 degrees (`MarmotRule.seenFrom`), so the animal is never hidden by a bend or a hairpin. It starts only when the animal will be off the road at least 1 s before the bike arrives at its present speed (`MarmotRule.mayStart`). A bike too fast for the sight line gets no crossing.
- **Fairness.** A marmot is never solid, and the physics has no knowledge of it. It cannot make the bike crash or stop, so a ride with a crossing and a ride without are equal, and no board seed is needed. There is no call-out and no bonus.
- **Checks.** `tests/unit/marmots.test.js` holds the rule on tiles built in memory, the crossing rule at every speed and the hiding. `node tools/terrain-check.mjs world-marmots --world tremola --gpu [--out <dir>] [--crossing --speed 12]` lists the colonies of the stage and checks each animal as drawn (on the drawn ground, past the road bands, out of the water). It takes pictures of a colony from 6, 25, 60 and 120 m, and with `--crossing` rides a forced crossing through the summit. `--ride` rides the summit in the chase view, counts the marmots drawn and the nearest to the camera, and saves the frames where one is nearest. `--watch` stands the bike 40 m from each colony for 10 s and fails a colony where no animal moves.
